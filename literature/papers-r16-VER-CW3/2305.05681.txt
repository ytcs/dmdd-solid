                                                          Detection of axion dark matter heavier than a meV is hindered by its small wavelength, which
                                                       limits the useful volume of traditional experiments. This problem can be avoided by directly detect-
                                                       ing in-medium excitations, whose ∼ meV − eV energies are decoupled from the detector size. We
                                                       show that for any target inside a magnetic field, the absorption rate of electromagnetically-coupled
                                                       axions into in-medium excitations is determined by the dielectric function. As a result, the plethora
                                                       of candidate targets previously identified for sub-GeV dark matter searches can be repurposed as
                                                       broadband axion detectors. We find that a kg · yr exposure with noise levels comparable to re-
                                                       cent measurements is sufficient to probe parameter space currently unexplored by laboratory tests.
                                                       Noise reduction by only a few orders of magnitude can enable sensitivity to the QCD axion in the
