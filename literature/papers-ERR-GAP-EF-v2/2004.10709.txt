                                                                The development of single charge resolving, macroscopic silicon detectors has opened a window into rare
                                                             processes at the O(eV) scale. In order to reconstruct the energy of a given event, or model the charge signal
                                                             obtained for a given amount of energy absorbed by the electrons in a detector, an accurate charge yield model is
                                                             needed. In this paper we review existing measurements of charge yield in silicon, focusing in particular on the
                                                             region below 1 keV. We highlight a calibration gap between 12–50 eV (referred to as the “UV-gap") and employ
arXiv:2004.10709v2 [astro-ph.IM] 24 Apr 2020




                                                             a phenomenological model of impact ionization to explore the likely charge yield in this energy regime. Finally,
                                                             we explore the impact of variations in this model on a test case, that of dark matter scattering off electrons, to
                                                             illustrate the scientific impact of uncertainties in charge yield.
