\chapter{Open problems}\label{chap:22}
A silicon detector sees dark matter with masses between 0.3332 and \SI{0.405}{MeV}, the first zero-phonon edge, only through the phonon-assisted rate, and that rate is known to a factor of ten (\cref{sec:13.9}). The zero-phonon edge of germanium is not known at all, and no calculation predicts how much charge a silicon recoil of \SI{100}{eV} releases (\cref{sec:19.2}). Each of these statements stands in the section where its quantity arises, next to the calculation or the measurement that would settle it.

Read one at a time, they do not say where effort pays. A calculation of a few hundred core-hours would turn that silicon estimate into a number with an error bar, while no calculation can sharpen a rate that is as uncertain as the $^{210}$Pb content of a crystal. Some statements of the book wait only for a proof and change no number; others are bounds that a single computation would close. We put these items side by side, each with the result it limits, and name the processes that the master formula does not cover; \cref{sec:22.1} orders the open calculations by the result each would change.
