\chapter{Pseudopotentials and core states}\label{chap:24}

A momentum transfer of \SI{30}{keV} resolves the electron density on a length scale $1/q = \SI{0.066}{\angstrom}$. That is smaller than the $2p$ shell of silicon, whose radial density peaks \SI{0.21}{\angstrom} from the nucleus, and it is the scale on which the valence states of silicon change sign twice to stay orthogonal to the core. Dark matter of a few MeV reaches such momentum transfers, and a heavy mediator, unlike a light one, does not suppress them against the few-keV momentum transfers of \cref{sec:1.3}. The density vertex $\rho_{mn}(\vect{k}, \vect{q})$ of \cref{eq:3.2-rho} at large $q$ is therefore a statement about the wavefunctions within a fraction of an angstrom of the nuclei. The calculations of \cref{chap:23}, on the other hand, are built to describe the bonds between the atoms, and they do so by throwing that region away.

\section*{Summary}

The states of a crystal are computed in plane waves with pseudopotentials (\cref{sec:3.5}). A vertex at momentum transfer $q$ needs plane waves up to about $q$ plus the momentum of the final state, far beyond the cutoff that converges a total energy. The cutoff that converges the energy of silicon to \SI{0.1}{meV} per atom leaves the density vertex at large momentum transfer unconverged. A pseudopotential replaces the core region by a smooth potential whose valence states agree with the true ones outside a core radius, and norm conservation fixes their charge there. Above about a keV, the density vertex depends on the oscillations of the valence states near the nuclei, which the all-electron reconstruction of the projector-augmented-wave method restores, and core and semicore shells are added as atomic orbitals where kinematics opens them. Relativistic corrections grow as $(Z\alpha)^2$ and stay at a few percent for every shell that dark matter lighter than about \SI{0.3}{GeV} can reach. A sum rule tested with a nonlocal Hamiltonian carries its nonlocal term (\cref{app:E.2}). For silicon the vertex beyond $q \sim 1/r_c$ is one of the dominant theory uncertainties of an electronic rate.
