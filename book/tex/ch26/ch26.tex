\chapter{Dielectric response and form factors}\label{chap:26}

The valence electrons of silicon, eight per primitive cell of volume $\Omega = a^3/4 = \SI{40.05}{\angstrom^3}$, have a plasma frequency $\omega_p = (4\pi\alpha\,\nd_e/m_e)^{1/2} = \SI{16.6}{eV}$, and the loss function $L(\vect{q},\omega)$ of \cref{sec:4.4} has its main peak near that energy at small momentum. Where exactly the peak is, how sharp it is, and how much spectral weight lies near the gap, where the dark-matter rate is largest for light masses, depend on how the response is computed. The bands and wavefunctions of \cref{chap:23,chap:24} enter an electron-recoil rate through the density vertices. Every electronic rate of \cref{chap:10} starts from the independent-particle form factor built from them, and screening and local fields use the same vertices as input.

\section*{Summary}

Every response calculation starts from the Adler--Wiser bubble of Kohn--Sham states. The random-phase dielectric matrix follows from it, local fields cost one matrix inversion per $(\vect{q},\omega)$, and time-dependent density-functional kernels and the Bethe--Salpeter equation on quasiparticle bands add exchange and the attraction of electron and hole. The wavefunction cutoff, the local-field cutoff, the band count, the $\vect{k}$ grid and the gap control the result and interact. In a semiconductor the $\vect{k}$ grid is usually the bottleneck. The band count is tested by the $f$-sum rule, and a scissor raises the first moment of $\varepsilon_2$ by $\Delta_{\mathrm{scis}}\int\dd\omega\,\varepsilon_2^{\mathrm{KS}}$. The independent-particle form factor of silicon up to \SI{30}{keV} is a sum of about $5\times10^{11}$ overlaps of plane-wave coefficients, some $10^{15}$ operations. The density vertex needs no correction for the nonlocal pseudopotential, while the velocity carries its commutator, which changes the absorption spectrum of diamond by 15 to 20 percent. The all-electron reconstruction is computed either with a second plane-wave cutoff or with radial integrals that have no cutoff. A form factor is tested at small $q$ against the velocity, against the sum rule of each occupied state, and against a higher cutoff with reconstruction.
