\chapter{Phonons from first principles}\label{chap:26}

Every number that \cref{sec:5.2,sec:5.3,sec:5.4,sec:5.5,sec:5.6} attach to the lattice of silicon, from the \SI{15.5}{THz} optical phonon at the zone center~\cite{nilsson1972study} to the Debye--Waller exponent at a momentum transfer of tens of keV, comes out of one kind of calculation: the first-order change of the Kohn--Sham potential when the nuclei of one species are displaced in a wave of crystal momentum $\vect{Q}$. For dark matter the result matters at both ends of the spectrum: the sound speeds that set the reach of acoustic-phonon searches (\cref{sec:9.2}) and the optical branches of polar crystals (\cref{sec:5.5}) all come out of density-functional perturbation theory (DFPT). The same linear-response equations that give a phonon also give, with an electric field or a strain in place of the displacement, the Born charges, $\varepsilon_\infty$ and the elastic constants, and, extended slightly, the changes of the empty states that a phonon-assisted amplitude needs (\cref{sec:13.2}). The calculation is for a harmonic crystal on a static lattice; the anharmonic and finite-temperature methods of \cref{app:E.8} all start from its harmonic phonons, Born charges and electron--phonon potentials.
