\chapter{Phonons from first principles}\label{chap:25}

Every number that \cref{chap:5} attaches to the lattice of silicon comes out of one kind of calculation, from the \SI{15.5}{THz} optical phonon at the zone center~\cite{nilsson1972study} to the Debye--Waller exponent at a momentum transfer of tens of keV. The calculation finds the first-order change of the Kohn--Sham potential when the nuclei of one species are displaced in a wave of crystal momentum $\vect{Q}$. For dark matter both ends of the spectrum matter, and density-functional perturbation theory (DFPT) gives both: the sound speeds that set the reach of acoustic-phonon searches (\cref{sec:9.2}) and the optical branches of polar crystals (\cref{sec:5.5}). The same linear-response equations that give a phonon also give, with an electric field or a strain in place of the displacement, the Born charges, $\varepsilon_\infty$ and the elastic constants, and, extended slightly, the changes of the empty states that a phonon-assisted amplitude needs (\cref{sec:13.2}). The calculation is for a harmonic crystal on a static lattice; the anharmonic and finite-temperature methods of \cref{app:E.8} all start from its harmonic phonons, Born charges and electron--phonon potentials.

\section*{Summary}

Force constants are second derivatives of the Born--Oppenheimer energy, obtained from finite differences of forces in a supercell or from the linear response of the electrons to a displacement wave. A frozen-phonon supercell of $N_Q^3$ cells gives exact phonons only on an $N_Q^3$ grid of wavevectors, at a cost that grows as $L^6$ to $L^9$ with the range $L$ of the force constants. DFPT solves the Sternheimer equation for the change of the occupied states, with no sum over empty states, and one perturbation costs about as much as the ground-state calculation; by the $2n+1$ theorem the first-order orbitals also fix the cubic constants. Fourier interpolation with the acoustic sum rule turns dynamical matrices on a coarse grid into the whole dispersion. The same runs give the Born charges and $\varepsilon_\infty$, which need the commutator of the Hamiltonian with the position operator, nonlocal term included; the potential changes that the electron--phonon vertex needs; and, by finite strain, the elastic constants. In silicon the $\vect{k}$ grid is fixed by the requirement that the Born charges vanish, as symmetry demands, and not by the frequencies. The quasi-harmonic and self-consistent harmonic approximations, TDEP, Allen--Heine--Cardona theory and special displacements all start from these harmonic phonons. For the zero-point renormalization of the silicon gap, nonadiabatic Allen--Heine--Cardona theory and special displacements give $-56$ and \SI{-57}{meV} at fixed volume. The two computed values fall about \SI{20}{\percent} short of the electron--phonon part of the measured renormalization, $-71$ to \SI{-73}{meV}, which remains once the contribution of the lattice expansion is removed.
