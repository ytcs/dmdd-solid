Silicon recoils of \SI{100}{eV}, below the binary threshold for ionization, are measured to produce charge, and no calculation yet predicts the yield (\cref{sec:19.2}). The measured pair energy and Fano factor of silicon are more precise than any first-principles cascade (\cref{sec:18.4}). No collection kernel has been computed for a real detector, and the readout conclusions of \cref{chap:20} stay conditional until the rates, kernels and efficiencies of a stated detector are computed. These calculations use the methods of \cref{chap:29}, and their costs run from seconds for the pair-number library to about $10^6$ core-hours for the close-collision kernel.

\section*{Summary}

The nuclear-recoil calculations give the close-collision excitation kernel on 80 points of collision energy and impact parameter and the partition of the recoil energy into heat, stored energy and charge as a joint distribution. They also give the charge yield on both sides of the binary threshold, \SI{352}{eV} in silicon and \SI{399}{eV} in germanium. Dense tracks add the interaction of their carriers, the recombination of the track plasma and the amorphous pockets of keV recoils. Molecular dynamics on machine-learned potentials computes all of these nuclear-recoil quantities except the excitation kernel and the track recombination. The electron cascades give the pair-number distribution $P_n(\vect{q},\omega,T)$ of silicon, germanium and GaAs, and then of the wide-gap and polar crystals that have no measured Fano factor, starting with sapphire, CaWO$_4$, LiF and NaF. A reduced cascade with energy-only branching gives the library of full distributions cheaply. In the readout window the pairs bind into excitons, are trapped or recombine at rates that are unknown in silicon and germanium, and the gap energy returns as phonons. The transport Monte Carlo then gives the collection kernels of a stated detector, the collection efficiencies that break the degeneracy of the silicon phonon-yield ratio 0.952, and the escape probability of a real film interface. A limit also needs the stream counts of the directional halo-independent bound, the cosmogenic activity of a crystal from its exposure history, and the spin expectation values of the nuclei that have a spin.
