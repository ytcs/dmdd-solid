\chapter{Detector response and inputs}\label{chap:32}

Silicon recoils of \SI{100}{eV}, below the binary threshold for ionization, are measured to produce charge, and no calculation yet predicts the yield (\cref{sec:19.2}). No first-principles cascade reaches the precision of the measured pair energy and Fano factor of silicon (\cref{sec:18.4}). No collection kernel has been computed for a real detector, and the readout conclusions of \cref{chap:20} stay conditional until the rates, kernels and efficiencies of a stated detector are computed. The sections of this chapter specify these calculations quantity by quantity, with the inputs, the grids and the convergence targets of each, using the methods of \cref{chap:29}; the last section does the same for the halo-independent bound of \cref{sec:21.2}, for cosmogenic production rates and for the nuclear inputs of spin-dependent scattering. Each calculation is accepted against an exact limit or sum rule and, wherever one exists, a measurement made at the same kinematics, never against the estimate it replaces. The costs run from seconds for the pair-number library to about $10^6$ core-hours for the close-collision kernel.
