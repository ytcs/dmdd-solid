The specifications of \crefrange{chap:30}{chap:32} end in tables: 12 real functions per $(\vect{q},\omega)$ for the electronic response of silicon, $1.2\times10^7$ values per quantity for its lattice response, generating functions on a grid of carrier energies for the cascade. A limit can be recomputed or recast from them only if their axes, conventions, versions and checks are declared, and \cref{sec:21.6} gives the schema. Most of the specified calculations have not been run. \Cref{sec:22.1} lists those that would change a printed result, limit or ranking, each with the check it must pass, and sorts the channels by what limits them: theory, calculation or data.

\section*{Summary}

Since a limit can be reproduced and recast only from the response tables it was computed with, a response library stores the response uncontracted, one function per pair of coupling rows and without a velocity axis, and declares with every table its truncation label, conventions, version and checks (\cref{sec:21.6}). Its next-order and alternative tables make the theory band of a limit reproducible. Of the 22 channels of \cref{fig:22-limits}, 12 are limited first by calculation, 9 by data and 2 by theory, the phonon-assisted window of silicon counting under both calculation and data (\cref{sec:22.1}). The first group holds the silicon electron recoil above \SI{1}{MeV}, the phonon-assisted window, the germanium edge and the Migdal rate below \SI{3}{MeV}. Their calculations are specified, and \cref{tab:22-calc} orders them by the result each would change; the phonon-assisted loss function of silicon, on common bins with the zero-phonon rate, changes the most. In the data-limited channels the properties of the sample or the detector have to be measured. In the two theory-limited ones, strongly anharmonic and quantum crystals at detector temperatures, and Dirac crystals at strong coupling with silicon doped near the Mott density, the next step is a new method.
