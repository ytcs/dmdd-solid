\chapter{Libraries and open calculations}\label{chap:33}

The specifications of \crefrange{chap:30}{chap:32} end in tables: 12 real functions per $(\vect{q},\omega)$ for the electronic response of silicon, $1.2\times10^7$ values per quantity for its lattice response, generating functions on a grid of carrier energies for the cascade. A limit can be recomputed or recast from them only if their axes, conventions, versions and checks are declared, and \cref{sec:21.6} gives the schema. Most of the specified calculations have not been run. \Cref{sec:22.1} lists those that would change a printed result, limit or ranking, each with the check it must pass, and sorts the channels by what limits them: theory, calculation or data.

\section*{Summary}

Every method of \crefrange{chap:23}{chap:29} starts from one base calculation, at clamped nuclei with independent particles, and moves one entry of the truncation label at a time. Every numerical parameter is converged on the quantity that is used, not on the total energy: the cutoff that converges the energy of silicon to \SI{0.1}{meV} per atom leaves the density vertex at large momentum transfer unconverged, and the $8^3$ grid that converges the energy to \SI{2}{meV} per atom leaves $\varepsilon_\infty$ nine percent too high. Three rules of bookkeeping recur: a measured gap already contains the zero-point renormalization; every quantity that enters one amplitude is computed in one gauge; and a sum rule tested with a nonlocal Hamiltonian carries its nonlocal term. The costs of the specifications run from seconds (the pair-number library) to about $10^6$ core-hours (the close-collision kernel). A library publishes the results with their truncation labels, conventions and checks, and \cref{tab:22-calc} orders the calculations still open by the result each would change.
