The electronic channels, with those that mix electrons with phonons or with the motion of a nucleus, are specified here with the methods of \crefrange{chap:23}{chap:25}, on the lattice base of \cref{app:E.17}.

\section*{Summary}

The phonon-assisted loss function of silicon is computed from the four-path amplitude, with the sum over intermediate states completed above the Wannier manifold by the Sternheimer remainder. It is computed on bins shared with the zero-phonon rate, so that the end of the phonon-assisted lead is settled as a ratio of two rates on the same bins. Its plane-wave stages cost some 350 to 450 core-hours. A bin converged to \SI{5}{\percent} is then tested against the measured indirect absorption in the optical limit and against the $f$-sum rule with the truncation and the nonlocal term separated. A germanium calculation first needs bands with the measured order of its valleys. Near the gap the finite-momentum Bethe--Salpeter equation with its coupling block adds the bound excitons, the exciton--phonon replicas and the exciton lines of the Migdal response, and the core levels and the spin--orbit mixing at the band edges complete the electronic response. A response library holds 12 real functions per $(\vect{q},\omega)$ for silicon, and the full independent-particle matrix on a coarse subset, from which the screened response to every operator follows by one Dyson equation; building the bubble dominates its cost, some $10^3$ core-hours. The nonadiabatic inputs, the drag masses and the crystal Migdal rate, are specified at the leading nonadiabatic order, the rate as a convolution of an electronic and a lattice response that no single code has yet evaluated. Coherent and second-order absorption use the same responses on other lines of the $(q,\omega)$ plane.
