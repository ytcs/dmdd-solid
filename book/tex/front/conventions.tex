\chapter*{Conventions and units}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conventions and units}
We use natural units, $\hbar = c = k_{\mathrm{B}} = 1$, unless stated otherwise, and quote physical values in conventional units with SI prefixes.

A widely used formula from the literature appears in a gray box called a Limit Check. The box compares the formula with a fuller result: a measurement, or a calculation in the book for a real crystal or for a model that keeps the physics the formula drops. The plot shows the ratio of the formula to the fuller result, or both as curves, against the \emph{controlling parameter}, along which their relative difference grows from negligible to order one. A dashed or dotted curve shows the \emph{leading correction}, the first term of that difference. A gray band marks where the formula agrees with the fuller result to the tolerance in the caption, usually \SI{10}{\percent}. Where the fuller result is a model, the edge of the band is an estimate, not a bound; where nothing is shaded, the formula fails over the whole plotted range. \Cref{lim:1.3a} is an example, and \cref{app:F} lists the most used Limit Check formulas.
