\chapter*{Conventions and units}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conventions and units}
We use natural units, $\hbar = c = k_{\mathrm{B}} = 1$, unless stated otherwise, and quote physical values in conventional units with SI prefixes.

A widely used formula from the literature appears in a gray box called a Limit Check, which plots the formula against a fuller result. A Limit Check has three parts: the formula, a plot, and one sentence. The formula is written in the book's notation, and a one-line citation names the equation of the source in which the formula was first given. The fuller result describes the same quantity as the formula and is either a measurement or a calculation in the book, for a real crystal or for a model that keeps the physics the formula drops. Most boxes plot the ratio between the formula and the fuller result, so that the formula is the horizontal line at one; some boxes plot the formula and the fuller result as two curves, when the shapes of the curves matter more than their ratio. The horizontal axis carries the \emph{controlling parameter}: along this axis the relative difference between the formula and the fuller result runs from negligible at one end to order one at the other. The \emph{leading correction} is the first term of this relative difference in the controlling parameter, and it is drawn as a third curve, usually dotted or dashed. A gray band marks the values of the controlling parameter at which the formula agrees with the fuller result, to a tolerance stated in the caption, usually \SI{10}{\percent}, and the caption prints the edge of the band. In \cref{lim:1.3a} the free-recoil formula $\omega = q^2/2M$ is compared with the energy deposited in a nucleus bound in an Einstein potential, as a function of the recoil energy in units of the phonon energy. The leading correction in that box is the rms width $(E_R\omega_0)^{1/2}$ of the deposited energy. The sentence under the plot says what breaks down outside the band. The running text next to the box derives the formula from the book's general result.

Inside the gray band the formula is established to the stated tolerance, with the same confidence as the fuller result. Where the fuller result is a model, the edge of the band is an estimate of where the formula fails, not a bound. Where nothing is shaded, the formula fails over the whole plotted range, and the box gives the size of the failure in place of a band edge. Some boxes compare two published forms of one result, or test a sum rule; these boxes are read in the same way, with the exact relation as the fuller result. \Cref{app:F} lists the most used of the Limit Check formulas with their source equations and the sizes of their leading corrections.
