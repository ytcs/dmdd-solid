\chapter{Lattice and special targets}\label{chap:31}

One harmonic calculation per crystal by the DFPT of \cref{chap:26}, about 100 core-hours for silicon, supplies every lattice channel of \cref{chap:11} for silicon, germanium and gallium arsenide (\cref{app:E.17}), and the electronic specifications of \cref{chap:30} start from the same ground state and phonons. The anharmonic and disordered solids of \cref{sec:11.5} and the targets of \cref{chap:14,chap:15,chap:16} have no such common base. Their specifications cost from CPU minutes, for the conserving response of clean aluminum and the ejection factors of graphene, to $10^5$ to $10^6$ hours on graphics processors for the two-level systems of a glass. At millikelvin the response of a quantum crystal is bounded only by the band of its exact moments (\cref{app:E.19}). The specifications of \crefrange{chap:31}{chap:32} share one rule: each calculation is accepted against an exact limit or sum rule and, wherever one exists, a measurement made at the same kinematics, never against the estimate it replaces.

\section*{Summary}

For silicon, germanium and gallium arsenide one harmonic base, force constants, eigenpairs, per-atom densities of states, displacement tensors and, for GaAs, $Z^*$ and $\varepsilon_\infty$, is computed once by DFPT. Its frequencies are accepted against neutron data at $\mathrm{\Gamma}$, X and L with a tolerance per branch, and the deformation potentials join it by frozen strain. The multiphonon tables follow from the per-atom density of states and are validated on an Einstein site and by the $f$-sum rule of the lattice to $10^{-4}$. Beyond the base, the vertex at large momentum needs all-electron densities, the impulse peak path-integral momentum distributions, the absorption by isotope disorder the Hessian of a disordered supercell, and the radiation of a recoiling ion the dynamical Born charge. For strongly anharmonic and quantum crystals the frequency moments of order 0 to 4 of the self response are exact from a static path-integral simulation, and glasses need melt-quench structures before they need responses. The magnetic, superconducting and Dirac targets need the parameters of their models, exchange constants from DFT~$+\,U$, Eliashberg functions and Dirac velocities, and the responses that go beyond them. Donor clusters, molecular excitons, electrons ejected from graphene, color centers and dissociating molecules have final states that the band picture does not contain. The parameters of tunneling defects and two-level systems, from the ohmic coupling and the release paths to the density vertex and the distribution of two-level parameters, have not been computed for a real material at the level the text needs.
