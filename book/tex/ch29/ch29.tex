\chapter{Recoils, transport and cascades}\label{chap:29}

What a detector records after an energy deposit is decided in three stages. A struck atom leaves the region where the force constants of \cref{sec:5.2} apply and may end as a vacancy and an interstitial that store several eV. A hot electron multiplies into electron--hole pairs by impact ionization while it loses energy to phonons. The phonons and charges that result then cross the crystal, over microseconds, before a sensor absorbs them. \Cref{chap:18,chap:19,chap:20} derive the physics of these stages. The numbers they need come from simulations that follow many trajectories: the displacement threshold and the stored energy from molecular dynamics, the collection kernels from a transport Monte Carlo, the pair energy and the Fano factor from a cascade Monte Carlo. Each simulation is followed here through silicon and placed on the truncation label $\mathfrak{T}$ of \cref{sec:8.5}, so that a number in the text can be traced to what it neglects and to the check that would expose it.
