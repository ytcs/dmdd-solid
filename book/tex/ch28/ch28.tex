\chapter{Magnets, superconductors and defects}\label{chap:28}

Silicon, the running example of the book, has neither magnetic order nor a Fermi surface; a spin-polarized calculation returns zero moment, and $\alpha^2F$ vanishes identically for an undoped insulator at $T = 0$ (\cref{sec:6.4}). The magnetic and superconducting targets of \cref{sec:7.1,sec:7.2} and of \cref{chap:14,chap:15} are worked out here for NiO and aluminum. Defect properties come from supercells whose defect must be isolated from its own images, electrostatically for charged states and dynamically for a recoil.

\section*{Summary}

In NiO semilocal functionals give a small gap of the wrong character, and DFT~$+\,U$ restores the charge-transfer gap. The Hubbard parameter is computed by linear response, \SI{8.17}{eV} for nickel with Löwdin-orthogonalized projectors, but it depends on the projectors, the functional and the pseudopotential, and published linear-response values for the same shell run from 4.6 to \SI{8.17}{eV}. Exchange constants follow from energy mapping of collinear configurations, from spin spirals, from the Green's-function formula of Liechtenstein and coworkers or from the poles of the spin susceptibility, and each depends on $U_{\mathrm{H}}$. In aluminum the linear-response $\alpha^2F$ gives $\lambda_{\mathrm{ep}} = 0.44$, and the Eliashberg equations reproduce the measured $T_c$ of \SI{1.18}{K} and gap of \SI{0.18}{meV} once the Coulomb pseudopotential is set to 0.12. A change of $\mu^*_{\mathrm{C}}$ by $\pm0.02$ moves $T_c$ by a factor of 0.60 or 1.53, so a computed $T_c$ is a consistency check on $\alpha^2F$ rather than a prediction. The spread over $U_{\mathrm{H}}$ and mapping, or over $\mu^*_{\mathrm{C}}$, is the theory uncertainty of a target ranked by a computed parameter. Defect energies come from supercells. A charged defect needs an image-charge correction that falls only as $1/L_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and is still \SI{80}{meV} for a singly charged defect in 512 atoms of silicon, and $\Delta$SCF gives the optical transitions of color centers whose states are single determinants. Shallow donors extend beyond any supercell and are treated in effective-mass theory.
