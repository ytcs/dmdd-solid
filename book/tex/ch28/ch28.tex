\chapter{Magnets, superconductors and defects}\label{chap:28}

Silicon, the running example of the book, has neither magnetic order nor a Fermi surface; a spin-polarized calculation returns zero moment, and $\alpha^2F$ vanishes identically for an undoped insulator at $T = 0$ (\cref{sec:6.4}). The magnetic and superconducting targets of \cref{sec:7.1,sec:7.2} and of \cref{chap:14,chap:15} are worked here on NiO and aluminum. Defect properties come from supercells whose defect must be isolated from its own images, electrostatically for charged states and dynamically for a recoil.
