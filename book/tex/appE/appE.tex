\chapter{Ab initio methods}\label{app:E}

A dark-matter rate in silicon needs a few dozen numbers about the crystal: a gap good to tens of meV, a loss function out to \SI{100}{eV}, density vertices at momentum transfers of tens of keV, electron--phonon couplings on grids of $10^9$ points, and the energy a struck atom leaves behind. Almost none of them is measured everywhere it is needed. Each is computed by a method with its own approximation, numerical parameters and typical failure. \Crefrange{app:E.1}{app:E.13} follow each method through one calculation, almost always of silicon, so that a number in the text can be traced to what it neglects and to the check that would expose it. \Crefrange{app:E.14}{app:E.27} go the other way and specify, quantity by quantity, the calculation behind each number the chapters use, with its inputs, its method and its convergence targets. Every method is placed on the truncation label $\mathfrak{T}$ of \cref{sec:8.5}, whose components include the Born--Oppenheimer order $n_B$, the electron--phonon order $n_g$, the anharmonic order $n_A$, the thermal order $n_T$, the many-body approximation $\mathcal{X}$, the lattice regime $\mathcal{K}$ (one phonon, multiphonon or impulse) and the Migdal regime $\mathfrak{M}$; a calculation at clamped nuclei, with independent particles and no phonons, sits at its base point $\mathfrak{T}_0$.

\section*{Summary}

Every method in this appendix starts from one base calculation, at clamped nuclei with independent particles, and moves one entry of the truncation label at a time. Every numerical parameter is converged on the quantity that is used, not on the total energy: the cutoff that converges the energy of silicon to \SI{0.1}{meV} per atom leaves the density vertex at large momentum transfer unconverged, and the $8^3$ grid that converges the energy to \SI{2}{meV} per atom leaves $\varepsilon_\infty$ nine percent too high. Three rules of bookkeeping recur: a measured gap already contains the zero-point renormalization; every quantity that enters one amplitude is computed in one gauge; and a sum rule tested with a nonlocal Hamiltonian carries its nonlocal term. For silicon the dominant theory uncertainties are few: the gap (about \SI{0.1}{eV}), the screening ($\varepsilon_\infty$ of 13.0 computed against 11.7 measured), the vertex beyond $q \sim 1/r_c$, and, for recoils, the interatomic potential. The rest are controlled numerically. The specifications of \crefrange{app:E.14}{app:E.27} share one rule: each calculation is accepted against an exact limit or sum rule and, wherever one exists, a measurement made at the same kinematics, never against the estimate it replaces. Their costs run from seconds (the pair-number library, the corrected Dirac reaches) to about $10^6$ core-hours (the close-collision kernel), and several of the cheapest, the verified onset of silicon, the boosted-dark-matter recast and the published-limit corrections, decide numbers the chapters quote today.
