\chapter{Wannier interpolation}\label{chap:27}

A conduction electron of silicon \SI{10}{meV} above the band minimum has a crystal momentum within $(2m^*_\perp\times\SI{10}{meV})^{1/2} = \SI{44}{eV}$ of $\vect{k}_{\mathrm{c}}$ in the transverse direction (\cref{tab:3-materials}), \SI{2}{\percent} of $2\pi/a$. A uniform grid that places a point in every such pocket needs about $|\vect{b}_i|/\SI{44}{eV} \approx 90$ points along each reciprocal primitive vector, close to $10^6$ in the zone, and a phonon-assisted rate pairs each of them with as many phonon wavevectors (\cref{sec:6.5,sec:13.3}). Every point would be a plane-wave diagonalization, and every pair a perturbation calculation. Such grids are affordable only because the Bloch states of a group of bands can be recombined into orbitals localized within a cell or two, the Wannier functions. In their basis the Hamiltonian couples nearby cells and little else, and its Fourier series returns the bands at any $\vect{k}$ by diagonalizing an $8\times8$ matrix. They are the computed tight-binding orbitals that \cref{sec:3.2} anticipated, orthonormal, with hopping integrals taken from a first-principles calculation instead of fitted.

\section*{Summary}

Wannier functions recombine the Bloch states of a group of bands into orthonormal orbitals localized within a cell or two. They depend on a gauge $U(\vect{k})$, which maximal localization of the spread $\Omega_W$ fixes, and disentanglement extends them to bands that cross others, as the conduction bands of silicon do. The Hamiltonian between them, $H^W(\vect{R})$, has short range, and its Fourier series interpolates the bands exactly when that range fits inside the supercell of the coarse grid, and with an exponentially small error otherwise. Every quantity interpolated together must be rotated with one gauge. Vertices are interpolated in the same way once their long-range dipole and quadrupole parts are split off, and the density vertex is rebuilt for each momentum transfer. Near an absorption edge an energy window around the band extrema cuts the number of pairs $(\vect{k},\vect{Q})$ on $40^3$ grids by four orders of magnitude.
