## Place in the book
- Part I (Foundations), Chapter 1 (Light dark matter), first section of the book.
- Purpose: establish that dark matter exists, what its gravitational evidence fixes, and why particle masses below a GeV are open.
- Word budget: 4,000. Write subsection by subsection. Each Write/Edit call adds at most ~2,500 words.

## Reader state on entry

They have no astrophysics beyond textbook level. Natural units are defined in Appendix A.1; this section makes the first use of them (in Worked example 1.1), so point the reader there.

## Learning outcomes (testable)
1. The reader can derive the circular-velocity curve v_c(r) = (G_N M_enc(r)/r)^{1/2} and explain what a flat curve implies for M_enc(r).
2. The reader can define the critical density ρ_crit and h, state the cosmological dark-matter density Ω_c h², and say which observations fix it.
3. The reader can explain why the particle mass is unconstrained over many decades, and locate the keV–GeV window on that range.
4. The reader can derive the freeze-out estimate ⟨σv⟩ ≈ 3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³ s⁻¹ from n⟨σv⟩ ≈ H_Hub, and use it to explain (Lee–Weinberg) why thermal dark matter below a few GeV needs a light mediator or a non-thermal history.

## Must cover, in this order
1. **Galactic rotation curves.**
   - Newtonian gravity of a spherical mass distribution and circular orbits (a recap, derived).
   - Enclosed mass M_enc(r); the flat-curve argument.
   - The isothermal sphere ρ(r) = v_c²/(4πG_N r²), derived.
   - Fig. 1.2, a schematic rotation curve with disc, gas and halo contributions.
2. **Worked example 1.1:** from v_c ≈ 220 km/s at 8 kpc to a local density of order 0.3 GeV cm⁻³, with the isothermal sphere. This is the first use of natural units.
3. **Cosmological evidence**, compactly:
   - ρ_crit and h, Ω_c h² = 0.120 ± 0.001 (Planck 2018);
   - what the CMB, large-scale structure and lensing fix;
   - what they leave open, namely the particle nature and the mass.
4. **The mass range.** Why gravity alone leaves the mass unconstrained over many decades, the keV–GeV window, and Fig. 1.1: the candidate mass range with the solid-state excitation energy scales.
5. **Freeze-out**, in a box of about 400 words:
   - the Hubble rate in the radiation era, H_Hub ∝ T²/M_Pl;
   - n⟨σv⟩ ≈ H_Hub, giving ⟨σv⟩ ≈ 3 × 10⁻²⁶ cm³ s⁻¹ (derive the estimate: state each step and each approximation);
   - the WIMP as a historical benchmark.
6. **Light dark matter.**
   - Lee–Weinberg, stated qualitatively, with its cited result.
   - Why sub-GeV thermal dark matter needs a light mediator, or freeze-in, or a non-thermal history. Mediator, freeze-out and freeze-in at the qualitative level only; Sect. 1.4 develops models.
   - Close by stating why solids are the natural targets: eV-to-meV excitation energies against the available kinetic energy. Forward pointer to Sect. 1.3.
