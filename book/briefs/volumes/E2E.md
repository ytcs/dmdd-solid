## What to look for (things a unit editor could not see)
1. **Repetition across chapters**: the same explanation, derivation, number with its motivation, worked example, figure or table given in two chapters of the volume (or in a chapter and an appendix of another unit). Keep it in the first place a reader meets it, point back from the later one.
2. **Order and threads**: a concept used before the chapter that defines it; a result promised forward and never delivered; a thread (screening, sum rules, the kinematic edge, the Born–Oppenheimer hierarchy, the silicon phonon-assisted window, from response to measured signal, theory uncertainty) opened and never closed, or closed twice; chapters whose order fights the dependencies.
3. **Consistency**: one quantity quoted with different values in different chapters (gap, plasma frequency, densities, sound speeds, masses, thresholds, cross-sections), one symbol with two meanings, two symbols for one quantity, units and conventions against Appendix A. Do not choose a value yourself when two disagree: if one is plainly a typo of the other (the same source cited) fix it; otherwise list it.
4. **Openings, maps and summaries**: each chapter opening motivates and connects to its true neighbours in this volume; no opening or summary still describes material that was removed or moved; chapter maps and their captions match the sections; summaries condense, never repeat sentences of the text.
5. **Removed material**: a sentence, table row, caption or open-problem entry that still refers to a model, box, figure or section another editor removed (grep the logs' lists of removed labels; check `\cref` targets exist with `grep -rn 'label{X}' tex/`).
6. **Volume-level shape**: a chapter that does another chapter's job; a section that belongs in another chapter of the volume; a chapter that no longer earns its place. Propose moves in your report; make them only within your block (see below).
7. **Prose**: residue that reads as process (referee replies, audits, "we checked", "as corrected"), signposting, and repeated forward pointers into later volumes (Addendum 3).

## How the review runs
- **Block readers** each take a block of consecutive chapters and read every file of it in order, start to finish. Before that, each reads the opening and the Summary of every earlier chapter of the volume (and of Volume I for Volumes II–III), so that it knows what the reader already has, and the volume's duplicate list (book/ledgers/REVIEWS/volumes/E2E-volN-dups.txt: passage pairs, file:line, found by script across the whole volume). Block readers may edit inside their block (same limits as COMMON.md: no new physics, change no equation, number, symbol or citation key, grep before removing any label). For a pair in the duplicate list or a problem whose other side is in another block, the later side in reading order is cut when you own it; otherwise write it to the volume's notes file (book/ledgers/REVIEWS/volumes/E2E-volN-notes.md: file, line, what, suggested fix) for the lead.
- **Structural reader** reads, for the whole volume, the table of contents (tex/volN.tex and the chapter drivers), every chapter driver (opening, map caption), every section's first paragraph, every Summary, and every table caption. Writes findings only (book/ledgers/REVIEWS/volumes/E2E-volN-structure.md): items 2, 4 and 6 above, ranked by importance, each with file, line and a concrete fix. The lead applies or assigns them.
- Each reader compiles every chapter of its block with `/home/user/dmdd-solid/book/tools/chapcheck.sh <unit> /tmp/e2e-<unit>` (exit 0, no undefined references, no multiply defined labels, no float too large), runs the lint on changed files, and writes a log section in book/ledgers/REVIEWS/volumes/E2E-volN.md (block, words before/after, each change, problems left for others).
- Never use git stash, checkout or reset; never edit outside your block; scratch files get names with your block in them.
- Final message at most 12 lines.
