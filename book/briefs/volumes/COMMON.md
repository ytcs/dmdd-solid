## What to do in your unit
Read every file of your unit in full, in order, before you cut anything. Then:

1. **Repair the seams of the move.** Fix every sentence that describes the old structure: "this appendix" for what is now a chapter (or the reverse), "Part II", "Part III", "the book" where "this volume" is meant, "the previous/next chapter" where the neighbour changed, chapter openings and chapter-map captions that walk through sections that have moved away, `\crefrange` between labels that are no longer contiguous or no longer in order, "Appendix E" named in words, "earlier"/"above"/"below"/"later" for material that now sits in another volume or in a different order. A reference to another volume by \cref is correct and stays.
2. **Remove duplication.** One explanation of each thing, in the place a reader meets it first; elsewhere a \cref. Cut: a derivation given both in a chapter and in its appendix (keep the full steps in one place, a one-line pointer in the other); a paragraph that re-explains what an earlier section of the same volume established (replace by a clause with \cref); the restatement of a boxed key result in prose; a recap at the end of a subsection; a summary that repeats sentences of the text rather than condensing them; a figure caption that repeats the paragraph next to it; the same number motivated twice. The list of near-duplicate passages that a script found in your unit is in your brief; treat it as a starting point, not the whole job.
3. **Cut fluff.** Apply the deletion test to every sentence: if deleting it loses no fact, number, equation, definition, named object or step of an argument, delete it. Cut signposting ("we now turn to", "as we have seen", "it is worth noting"), announcements of what a section will do when the section then does it, hedges, and process residue (sentences that read as a reply to a reviewer, consistency assurances, justifications of a choice of presentation).
4. **Straighten threads.** Where an argument is interrupted by a digression, a forward reference stands in for a step, the same topic is treated in two separated passages of one section, or a result is used before the paragraph that derives it, reorder or merge within your unit so that each step follows from the last. Report (do not fix) a tangle that needs a change outside your unit.

## Limits (hard)
- **No new content.** No new physics, equation, number, citation, figure or claim. New words are allowed only as connective tissue: a transition, a pointer, a chapter opening assembled from sentences that already exist.
- **Do not change** any equation, number, unit, symbol, sign, citation key, cited equation number, figure file or label. Do not paraphrase a technical statement into a weaker or stronger one. Notation follows NOTATION.md and Appendix A in every volume; if you see an inconsistency, report it, do not "fix" it.
- **Never cut** a step of a derivation that is given only once, a definition, a stated assumption or domain of validity, a number with its source, an open problem, a Limit Check box (`limitbox`), a key result, or a worked example, unless the identical content stands elsewhere and you leave a pointer to it. When in doubt, keep.
- **Before deleting any `\label`,** run `grep -rn 'the:label' /home/user/dmdd-solid/book/tex --include=*.tex` (also check comma lists). If anything outside the passage refers to it, keep the labelled object or retarget those references inside your own unit only; report references in other units.
- Stay inside your unit's files. Do not edit preamble.tex, macros.tex, the bibliography, vol*.tex, appB (generated), files of other units, tools or ledgers. Other editors are working on the other units at the same time.
- House style is book/STYLE.md (read it; its rules on openings, titles, the master rate, plain words and looped restatement all stand) and the reader is book/READER.md (a first-year PhD student: keep every step and definition that persona needs). Titles are short noun phrases.
- Do not mention volumes, the restructuring, editors or any production process in the book's text.
