                                                         We propose a method for detecting single chiral phonons that will enable their use as dark-matter
                                                       detectors. We suggest metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) as detector materials, as their flexibility
                                                       yields low-energy chiral phonons with measurable magnetic moments, and their anisotropy leads to
                                                       directional sensitivity, which mitigates background contamination. To demonstrate our proposal, we
                                                       calculate the phononic structure of the MOF InF3 (4, 4′ -bipyridine), and show that it has highly chiral
                                                       acoustic phonons. Detection of such chiral phonons via their magnetic moments would dramatically
                                                       lower the excitation energy threshold for dark matter detection to the energy of a single phonon.
                                                       We show that single phonon detection in a MOF would extend detector reach ten or more orders of
                                                       magnitude below current limits, enabling exploration of a multitude of as-yet-unprobed dark matter
