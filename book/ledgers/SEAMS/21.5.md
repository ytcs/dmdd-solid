## The reader now knows
- Unknown background: counting every event as signal is valid for any N_bkg ≥ 0 (dP(N ≤ n|μ)/dμ = −P(N = n|μ)); limits 2.303, 3.890,
  5.322, 6.681 for 0–3 events.
- Maximum gap (Yellin Eq. (2), eq:21.5-C0): positions measured in expected signal counts; C_0(x, N_sig) shape-independent; limit from
  C_0(gN, N) = 0.9 with g the expected-signal fraction in the largest gap; zero events → 1 − e^{−N} → 2.303; conservative under any
  background (added events only split gaps). One event: g = 0.9 → 2.836 (vs 3.890), g = 0.5 → 7.78; break-even g = 0.761. Two events in
  the lowest tenth → 2.836 (vs 5.322); in thirds → 13.8. Method fixed before data.
- Optimum interval (Yellin Sect. III), stated: C_n, C_Max over (N+1)(N+2)/2 intervals, calibration C̄_Max(0.9, N) by Monte Carlo.
  Needs an ordered continuous variable; integer pair bins → Poisson per bin + minimum over bins.
- No likelihood → theory nuisances cannot be profiled with Yellin's methods: normalization band = envelope (first order); tilt changes g.
- Combination (eq:21.5-comb): product of eq:8.8-like over experiments X, one ϑ_k shared, detector nuisances ϑ^{det,(X)} per experiment.
  Why shared (8.8). Steps: one response at one truncation label (tabulation spread = one shared nuisance, 10.10); conventions (ρ_χ, halo,
  step-model gap) removed, not profiled; one halo for all (near the edge only the 21.3 combinations); cross-material: one ϑ_k, own δ_k.
- WE 21.5 (we:21-combine): 10 MeV heavy, book halo, 2-pair bin [4.71, 8.31) eV (step model 1.11/3.6, as 21.2), r = 7.97e41
  /(kg yr cm²); A 1 kg yr, 0 events; B 4 kg yr, 3 events, b = 2.5; δ = 0.25 normalization. Exact Neyman construction (enumeration ≤ 60
  each), one-sided profile statistic, nuisance at ϑ̂̂_obs, constraint fixed. A 2.89e-42, B 1.31e-42, combined 0.928e-42 (0.32 of A,
  0.71 of B); shared nuisance 1.029e-42 (×1.109, ϑ̂̂ = −0.46); per-exposure 0.975e-42 (×1.051; −0.17/−0.29); envelope ×1.25. One
  zero-count exposure: N_nom = N_90 e^{k²N_90} (×1.12), against 1.06 from 8.8's calibrated threshold.
- Recast: 𝒦_j = r_j^pub/r_j^new (eq:21.5-K). Keyresult eq:21.5-recast: common factor → exact for any procedure; bin-dependent → exact only
  for a minimum over bins with N_j^up public (incl. single bin); bracket eq:21.5-bracket min𝒦 ≤ ratio ≤ 𝒦_{j*} ≤ max𝒦; PLR/optimum interval
  not recastable from the curve.
- Coverage of a minimum over n bins at 90 %: 0.9^n (0.81, 0.73); Bonferroni 1 − 0.1/n, ln(10n) (3.401 for three).
- Halo recast example (Essig 230/600/240, ρ 0.4 → book): 10 MeV 𝒦 = 1.294/1.222/1.150 (0.971/0.917/0.863 at fixed ρ); 3.49e-42 →
  4.27e-42 (bin 2, ×1.222); bracket 1.150–1.294. 2 MeV: 1.139/1.473 (0.854/1.105); v_max 840 vs 794.5.
- Table tab:21-recast: ρ_χ, normalization nuisance, isotropic Dirac background (common); halo shape, screening/tilts, gap shift
  (bin-dependent); anisotropic background (q̂-dependent; event integrals); boosted relativistic row (speed-dependent; R^{−1/2}).
- Minimal publication for exact recasting (per-bin counts, backgrounds+covariance, exposure, efficiency, detector response, N_j^up + CL
  convention, halo, truncation label, machine-readable likelihood); response side → 21.6.
- Dirac: isotropic ε_bg² (σ̄_e), ε_bg^{1/2} (ζ), common → exact for any procedure and normalization; no extra charge-renormalization factor
  (D.41). Fig. 21.5a: Hochberg ideal material ×1600 (gapped minimum 3.4e-46 near 9.5 keV → 5.4e-43; gapless 114 keV 5.5e-45 → 8.8e-42).
  Fig. 21.5b: anisotropic ZrTe₅ (11.8 meV row, book halo, orientation average) factor 5200–6400 (20 keV–1 MeV), 2200 at 10 keV, 820

## Open questions / for the editor
1. **Construction consistency with 21.4.** WE 21.5 uses an exact Neyman construction (enumeration) with the one-sided profile statistic,
   the nuisance at its conditional best fit and the constraint held fixed. 21.4 (drafted in parallel) discusses two other constructions
   (calibrated threshold, coefficient 1.15; constraint-averaged probability, 0.65); the WE names the third (coefficient N_90 = 2.30) in one
   sentence against those two. If the editor wants one construction chapter-wide, rerun we21_5.py (~30 s); the qualitative results
   (shared cost ≈ twice the per-exposure cost, second order in k) do not depend on it.
2. **8.8 cost vs the exact construction.** For one zero-count exposure the exact construction gives N_90 e^{k²N_90} (1.12 at δ = 0.25),
   8.8's calibrated threshold t* = 4.61 gives 1.06 (21.4 also quotes 0.65 for the constraint average). Stated once, with sec:21.4-like.
3. **10.9 text now partly superseded.** s10-9 (l.127, limit box closing sentence, sec:10.9-open) says the effect on the published CRDM limits
   is open/unknown. With REF-DC-49-54-36 §4 items 1–3 (validated, one referee) the heavy-mediator limit is unchanged and the light-mediator
   one is settled to < 1 % above 10 keV; only < 3 keV stays open. 21.5 quotes items 1–3 in their substance; I dropped the phrase "which we
   could not reproduce" (process wording, Ch13 R6). 10.9 should be aligned at its next pass.
4. **Pair-bin model.** As in 21.2 (step model 1.11/3.6 eV), for consistency within the chapter; the 21.2 editor note asks whether to move to
   the Ch18 calibration (3.75 eV). If changed, rerun we21_5.py and stats21_5.py §4.
5. **δ = 0.25** in WE 21.5 is the size quoted in 10.10's text for the largest silicon truncations at 10 MeV ("a quarter"), used as one
   normalization nuisance for illustration.
6. **Anisotropic ZrTe₅ recast factor** is the 16.1 computation (zrte5_16.npz); 16.1 prints 5200–6400 over 20 keV–1 MeV, and fig21_5.log
   reproduces it. The near-threshold values (2200, 820) and their explanation (y axis, ε_yy² = 431) are new here.
7. Symbol 𝒦_j is distinct from 𝒦_t (Fejér kernel, 14.6) by its subscript; editor may prefer another letter.
