## Open questions / for the editor
1. **Sign of 𝔯_coll − 1.** 19.5 (l.154) says transport estimates give 𝔯_coll at or slightly below one and the sign changes only if a W
   sensor or another film absorbs sub-gap phonons. The book's mean-field estimate agrees for sub-gap loss with flat yields, but finds the
   near-gap film yield (2Δ/E up to 4Δ) of the same size and either sign: it can push 𝔯 to 1.04–1.09 when the Luke spectrum reaches the
   higher-gap film's threshold. Suggest 19.5 adds "and the film yield near the threshold" (one clause) at its revision. The near-gap ramp
   4Δ→6Δ (0.5 → 0.59, linear) is a modelling choice; 20.4's final Y_qp numbers should be checked against it.
2. **ℭ_s definition.** E.25 defines ℭ_s as the fraction "that sensor s absorbs"; 20.5 defines it as the fraction ending as quasiparticles
   (absorption × Y_qp), which is what 𝔯_coll (an efficiency of signal) needs. Align E.25 by one word at the App. E pass.
3. **ϖ_O range.** E.25 quotes the mean-field ϖ_O as 0.1–0.4. The book estimate gives 0.41 for ⟨E_L⟩ ≲ 1.5 meV, falling to 0.20 at 2 meV,
   0.08 at 2.9 meV and 0.03 at 4.7 meV (spread shape). The campaign toy's 0.42/0.28/0.11 used a flat-energy spectrum truncated at a grid
   edge; its 𝔯_coll = 0.87–0.98 came mostly from that truncation (sub-gap share 0.118 at 0.75 meV, against 0.017–0.058 here).
4. **HV ceiling numbers differ from the campaign's** (1.9986/2.014 at a constructed 300 eV envelope). The book uses only measured rows,
   log-linear interpolation, the book's measured-charge Fano factor (binomial) and the mean-charge limit, and f_det ∈ {0.873, 1}; it finds
   1.72 as the 1σ maximum (220 eV) and 2.46 as the 2σ maximum (100 eV). The verdict (conditional; holds at 1σ, fails at 2σ) is the same.
5. ch20.tex opening (20.1's) says "Luke phonons collected 15 % better ... explain the datum": with the revised 19.5 (collection-only 1.128
   in bal19_5.log) this should read about 13 %; 20.5 writes "more than 0.1 above one".
6. Summary parts resting on SPINE only: the 20.2 paragraph (memory-loss times, rates ∝ E⁵, E⁴, scaling form, sapphire) and the 20.4
   paragraph (Y_qp forms, Kaplan cascade, t_l/t₀, soft recombination phonons), and the corresponding nuisance rows (memory loss; Y_qp
   above 4Δ: 0.57–0.6 and the 40 % thin-film reduction taken from 1401.1937 Sect. V). Recheck against the final s20-2/s20-4.
7. 20.1 uses F_eh = 0.114 (cascade) in its Fano example; 20.5 uses the measured 0.119 for electron recoils (as 18.4's measured value).
