## The reader now knows

  positive-frequency weight at k = −m_φv (sign of eq:A.3-space/time), while the target receives p_φ = +m_φv (O(+m_φv)); k-integrated
  (πρ_χ/m_φ²)F_φ(ω); F_φ dω = F_χ dv (eq:14.6-Fphi, the 11.8 row); one-sided line ending 3.5e-6 above m_φ.

  noted once (Budker; Centers). Standard halo seen from the Earth: F_φ peak 0.58/t_coh, mean 1.27/t_coh, equivalent width

  (1/m v₀)³ at 1 neV.
- Golden rule as a record-length limit: Gaussian average of the absorbed quanta = t(ρ/m)× eq:8.2-abs averaged over F_φ with
  δ(ω − E_f + E_i) → Fejér kernel 𝒦_t (eq:14.6-fejer); holds for t ≫ min(t_coh, 1/Γ_T); absorbed energy ∝ t² below both. Single
  realization ≈ mean needs t ≫ t_coh. Counting statistics: geometric within one coherence time, Var N = N̄ + N̄²/N_coh (bunching);
  Poisson for N_coh ≫ N̄ (YIG WE 14.3: 35 events/h in 3.4e7 coherence times).

## Open questions / for the editor
1. **t_coh convention clash in E.26.** E-26.tex l.3 writes t_coh = 2π/(m_φv₀²) ≈ 6.6 ms at 1 µeV; the book definition (NOTATION, 8.2,
   11.8, 14.3, Ch11 ruling R7, now eq:14.6-tcoh) is 1/(m_φv₀²) = 1.04 ms. E.26 l.7/l.11 ("t_int ... 10⁻² to 10³ t_coh") inherit it.
   Fix E.26 (outside my files).
2. **8.2 / D.25 wording.** They say the golden rule needs t ≫ t_coh AND t ≫ 1/Γ_T. 14.6 states, positively, that the Gaussian-average
   mean needs only t ≫ min(t_coh, 1/Γ_T), and that a single realization matches the mean for t ≫ t_coh. Not contradictory (8.2 speaks of a
   single realization), but a light pass could align 8.2's sentence.
3. **Summary paragraphs for 14.4 and 14.5** were written while those sections were in progress, from the SPINE and the parts then on disk
   (s14-4 keyresults; s14-5 headings). Recheck at the chapter review: 14.4 (no neutron route to q̂·S·q̂; isotropy inversion from two
   angles; C_sn(0) = 0.83 for Ni²⁺, g = 2.2) and 14.5 (finite-q optical activity, point-group test, chiral phonons, SMMs, multiferroic
   below T_CM). The four nuisance rows for 14.4/14.5 (conversion factor, longitudinal response, chiral-framework branching/giant moments,
   multiferroic near T_CM) likewise follow the SPINE open-problem fields; adjust sizes/basis if those sections size them.
4. **Limit Check 14.6c model.** The exact Neyman curves replace the Fejér-convolved halo line by N_eff equal bins (participation number).
   The single-bin and long-record limits are exact; the transition region (t_coh to 100 t_coh) depends on that box model, which the caption
   and F row state. The crossover location depends on the statistic (median limit vs threshold data), so the text says the coefficient is
   not universal, consistent with the research note that "(3/2)t_coh" is an artefact.
5. **Limit Check 14.6a/b controlling parameters.** SPINE's LIMITS table lists x = t/T₂ for 14.6a and t_int/t_coh for 14.6b. 14.6a is
   plotted against t/t_coh (the validity parameter, t ≪ t_coh) with two T₂ values; y is normalized by T₂. 14.6b combines the Foster
   spectrum (mean periodogram) and the golden-rule mean of absorbed energy, which coincide for T₂ → ∞.
6. **Fixed-amplitude factor.** The book's own model gives 1.99 (median 95 % limit, one bin); Centers et al. give 2.7–3.0 (threshold
   data). Both are printed, with the reason for the difference. SPINE/CW3 "penalty 2.7–10" stands as the literature value.
7. **1306.6089 equation numbering.** The arXiv PDF prints the supplement scalings as Eqs. (A7), (A8) (the research note's "(8)–(9)" is
   the HTML numbering). One scheme used: PDF.
