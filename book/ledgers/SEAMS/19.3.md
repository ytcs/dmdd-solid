## The reader now knows
- Lindhard quenching (y_L) is distinct from the Lindhard function ε_Lin of 4.5 (one sentence, opening).
- Binary-collision model of a slow ion: Thomas–Fermi screened potential, screening length a_L = 0.8853a₀/(√2 Z^{1/3}) = 0.1375 Å
  (Si); reduced energy ϵ_L = c_L E, c_L = a_L/(2Z²e_G²) = 0.02435 keV⁻¹ (= 11.5 Z^{-7/3}) (eq:19.3-reduced); ϵ_L = 1 at 41 keV.
- Scattering function f_LSS(x_L), x_L = ϵ_L sin(θ_s/2), dσ_n = πa_L² f_LSS dx_L/x_L² (eq:19.3-dsigma); derived from the impulse
  deflection, energy-independent; Rutherford 1/2x_L; computed peak 0.43 at x_L = 0.14; reduced nuclear stopping s_n peaks 0.41 at
  ϵ_L ≈ 0.3.
- Friction law dϵ_L/dρ_L = k_Lϵ_L^{1/2}, k_L = 0.133Z^{2/3}A^{-1/2} = 0.146 (eq:19.3-Se), hand-inserted Z^{1/6}; physical stopping
  0.88/2.77/8.76 eV/Å at 0.1/1/10 keV (Si in Si). Empirical at recoil speeds: Born parameter 14.6; first-order (Z_κ² × electron-gas)
  stopping would be 64 eV/Å at 1 keV, 23× the k_L value.
- Lindhard's basic equation as the first-moment (first-collision) equation of the binary branching process, from the partition of
  19.1 with E_st = 0 (eq:19.3-basic); damage energy T_d(E) as function of starting energy; approximations (A)–(E); the simplified
  equation in reduced units (eq:19.3-simple), boundary ϵ_d → ϵ_L.
- Small-ϵ law y_L ≃ (2k_L/λ_LJ)ϵ_L^{1/6}, J = 0.4742 (eq:19.3-asym); with λ_L = 1.283 the coefficient is 3.29k_L — origin of
  3ϵ^{0.15} in g_L.
- Numerical solution of eq:19.3-simple (Thomas–Fermi impulse f, x^{1/3} continuation below 2e-3): Lewin–Smith fit within 3 % below
  100 eV, 4–5 % low from 1–20 keV (0.210 vs 0.220 at 1 keV), ≤ 5 % for ϵ ≥ 0.1. Continuation at the local slope 0.363 raises the
  solution by 13 % (1 keV) and 10 % (4 keV): the keV yield depends on soft collisions of the slowest secondaries.
- Three implicit assumptions: (i) no stored energy (U = 0 ⇒ E_st = 0); constant-binding model cuts off at 298 eV (U = 0.15 keV),
  contradicted by the 100 eV datum; variable U(E) = U_FP + U_EDFT model (χ²/dof 1870/101, 0.68 keV–3 MeV, threshold just above
  U_FP = 23.6 eV); secondary binding booked electronic by complement; (ii) friction to zero speed despite the binary threshold;
  the ½k_Lϵ^{3/2}ϵ_d'' term contradicts (D); (iii) proportional conversion Y_q = y_L, full collection, no trapping.
  Named approximations: random medium, no channeling; independent binary collisions; reduced-energy convention ±10 % (Z₁ = 12).
- Fig. 19.3: Lindhard (k = 0.146, 0.161), constant-binding ansatz, data, power law. Lindhard adequate above ≈ 4 keV.
- Bethe stopping from the loss function, derived from eq:4.4-dEdx by the q₀ split (keyresult eq:19.3-bethe, eq:19.3-lnI);
  matches PDG Eq. (34.5) NR limit (bracket + O(v⁴), prefactor 4πα²n_e/m_ev²).
- WE 19.3: valence single oscillator Ī_val = 20.3 eV (17 % above Ē = 17.4 eV, damping tail); measured Si loss function (Franta +
  Henke): band weights 0.280/0.607/0.113, log-means 19.5 eV/246 eV/3.17 keV, Ī = 161 eV (own f-sum, data f-sum 1.5 % high) to 174 eV
  (exact (π/2)ω_p²), adopted 173.0 eV; L shell carries two thirds of ln Ī.
- Limit Check 19.3b: electron gas (Mermin, γ = 0.1ω_p, Ī = 17.9 eV): Bethe within 10 % for v ≥ 1.84v_F, 5 % ≥ 2.27v_F, 1 % ≥ 4.3v_F;
  log vanishes at 0.60v_F; friction 1173 eV/Å × v (Z₁ = 1); Si recoils at 0.013–0.13v_F, Born 117–1170. PDG: corrected Bethe down to
  β ≈ 0.05; no satisfactory theory 0.01–0.05.
- Why tracks are outside scope.
