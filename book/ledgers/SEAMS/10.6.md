## Open questions / for the editor
1. **3.5 vs Griffin on the dominance mass.** 3.5 says the 3d electrons dominate the Ge heavy-mediator rate for m_χ ≳ 10 MeV
   (citing Griffin Sects. 1, 3.3). Griffin Sect. 4.1 / Fig. 13 and the Conclusions give ≳ 30 MeV for a one-pair threshold (Sect. 1's
   "≳ 10 MeV" refers to Fig. 2, which shows higher thresholds too). 10.6 states 30 MeV at one pair, lower at higher thresholds;
   suggest 3.5 be aligned.
2. **E.16 orbit–orbit ratio.** E.16 prints orbit–orbit ≈ 0.27 of mass–velocity, totals −0.23 % (Si), −0.73 % (Ge) (Thomas–Fermi), and
   "including only one misstates the total by about 21 %". The all-electron atomic estimate here gives 0.31–0.32, −0.24 %/−0.76 %, so
   ≈ 24 %. 10.6 quotes its own value and names E.16's as the Thomas–Fermi estimate; E.16 (and E.15's "−0.58 %" acceptance figure,
   which is δ_mv alone) may want updating.
3. **Ge 3d binding.** 3.5 uses 29 eV, E.15 "29.5 eV"; XDB gives 29.2 (5/2) / 29.8 (3/2), centroid 29.44. 10.6 uses the doublet
   and the centroid 29.4.
4. **Ge 3d ⟨v²⟩.** 3.5 (from ⟨r⟩, hydrogenic) 1.2e-3; direct ⟨p²⟩/m² 1.6e-3. Both stated in 10.6 with the reason.
5. **Ge HF total energy** was not available in an open text; K̄(Ge) uses the nonrelativistic all-electron PBE kinetic energy from the
   ld1.x atom (Si check: 561.3 vs 561.4 eV from 4.3's HF virial).
6. **E_ee** is estimated as E_H + E_xc of the PBE atoms (correlation included; < 1 % effect). The q→small limit counts every
   correlated intra-atomic pair; flagged in the text as the small-q value only.
7. **Model figure.** Fig. 10.5's core curves are free-atom plane-wave estimates; the valence switches from the KKL crystal table
   (TB09+scissor, 3d frozen) to the atomic model at 15 keV. The 3d/valence rate ratios in WE 10.6 bracket Griffin's result
   (plane waves below, Fermi factor above). A crystal c→c table (e.g. EXCEED-DM/Griffin data) would replace the bracket.
8. **Ge 3d band flatness**: no number for the crystal 3d bandwidth is quoted (Griffin footnote 2 checks it qualitatively).
