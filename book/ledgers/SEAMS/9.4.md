## The reader now knows
- Anatomy of a Limit Check: formula in book notation + one-line citation of the source equation; a comparison plot of the formula
  against a fuller result for the same quantity, which is the response one step further along one axis (𝔗 → 𝔗_k⁺ of 8.8; the plot shows
  δ_k of eq:8.8-delta, mostly as the ratio 1 + δ_k or its inverse); the controlling parameter on the x-axis; the leading correction as a
  third curve; a gray band where the two agree to the caption's tolerance (usually 10 %, sometimes 1 %, 15 %, or a width criterion), its
  edge found by a root-finder; one sentence on what fails outside. Worked through on Limit Check 1.3a (the reader's first box).
- Where the chain and the record live: approximation chain in the running text next to the box; formal record in tab:F-rows.
- Status of a formula read from its box: established to the stated tolerance inside the band, relative to the fuller result; an estimate
  of the failure point when the fuller result is a model (Einstein well of 1.3a); nothing shaded = fails over the plotted range, the box
  gives the size; sum-rule and two-published-forms boxes read the same way.
- Placement rule: read 𝔗 from assumptions (as WE 8.7); deviation notation; leading correction = largest step 𝔗 → 𝔗_k⁺; controlling
  parameter = what makes that step grow: the Table 8.4 parameter (x_R for impulse, ξ_D for dipole order), and on 𝒳 (no small parameter)
  the variable along which screening changes (q, ω, m_φ). Qualifiers (8.5's "in words") in four kinds: long wavelength; kinematics of halo
  and signal (incl. field coherence time); dark-matter side and coupling order; target model and method.
- Counts (fig9_3.log): 75 Part II Limit Checks (Ch10 15, Ch11 20, Ch12 9, Ch13 2, Ch14 8, Ch15 3, Ch16 17, Ch17 1); 27 on a label
  component (𝒳 12, 𝒦 6, n_A 3, 𝔐 3, n_D 2, n_T 1, n_B 0, n_g 0); 48 on qualifiers (long wavelength 5, kinematics/halo 9, DM side 5,
  target model 29); per chapter on the label: Ch12 7/9, Ch10 8/15, Ch11 7/20, Ch14 0/8, Ch16 2/17. App. F: 90 rows with home in
  Ch10–17, 80 boxed, 10 no-box, 6 unsized. The only n_g = 4 next step is the no-box phonon-assisted electron-recoil row (13.3).
- WE 9.4 (we:9-place): crystal form-factor rate at 𝔗₀ unscreened, leading step 𝒳, x = q/q₀, 0.05 / 0.22 / 0.47 (Limit Check 10.1a,
  nothing shaded); loss-function rate, 𝒳 untruncated (eq:8.4-exact), truncation = clamped lattice for an electron-only coupling, step
  𝒦 n/a → one-phonon (electron-following sector), x = ω/ω_max, >10⁴ inside the band in a model (10.2a); Lewin–Smith/Helm free recoil,
  𝒦 = impulse at zero width, n_B = 0, mean exact at every n_A, n_T, step = Doppler width x_R^{−1/2}, x = x_R, within 10 % for x_R ≥ 2.3
  (q ≥ 70 keV) at 1 GeV (11.4a); 1.3a vs 11.4a: same axis, bands at x_R ≥ 9 vs ≥ 2.3 because of the quantity compared (single-q spectrum
  width vs rate at fixed ω summed over q, width at second order when the mean is exact).

## Differences between SPINE and the book (followed the book)
1. SPINE title "Limitboxes" uses a banned word; the section is "Limit Checks and the truncation label".
2. SPINE LO (i) says "the reading of a box is in the front matter". The front matter (front/conventions.tex) has no reading of a box and
   no status words; 9.4 gives the reading in full (9.4.1). If a front-matter reading is added later, 9.4.1 should point to it or the
   front matter should point here.
3. SPINE prerequisite "App. F.1" does not exist (App. F is one table); 9.4 cites app:F and tab:F-rows.
4. SPINE "status words of the front matter": none exist; 9.4 uses only established-to-tolerance / estimate (model fuller result) /
   unsized, consistent with Ch22's classes, and points to chap:22 for the gaps.
5. Ch11 has 20 boxes (not 22 as a plain grep of chapters including 11.x suggests); Part II has 75; App. F has 90 Part II rows.
6. The placement is by the axis of the LEADING CORRECTION (the step 𝔗 → 𝔗_k⁺ that the box plots), not the full 𝔗 point of each
   formula; the full point is in each F row. This is the reading that predicts the x-axis (LO iii).
