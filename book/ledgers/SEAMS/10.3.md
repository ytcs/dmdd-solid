## Open questions / for the editor
1. **q_c value.** The spine says "q_c ≈ 2.24 keV in silicon"; 4.5 printed q_c = 2.60 keV (exact Lindhard zero on ω₊). Kept 2.60 keV
   as the book's q_c; 2.24 keV is stated as the cutoff of the quadratic (literature) dispersion, with 1.88, 2.38 and 2π/a = 2.28 keV.
   The F-row carries 2.24 for the formula and 2.60 for the fuller result.
2. **"(5/1.9)² refuted (≈ 1.4)".** With the book's cutoffs the logarithmic factor is 1.24 (2.60 keV) or 1.31 (2.24 keV); ≈ 1.4
   needs a 1.9 keV cutoff (the constant-ω_p value 1.88 keV). The text gives 1.24/1.31 and contrasts (5/2.60)² = 3.7 as "a measure
   growing like q". The text does not call the (5/1.9)² enhancement "often quoted": no public source for it was found.
3. **Kurinsky et al. used germanium** (E_p = 16 eV, EDELWEISS). The silicon numbers (2π/a = 2.28 keV, q_min = 0.17 keV) are the
   book's application of their formula to silicon, and the text says "for silicon".
4. **Z_L normalization** (the planning notes had it unresolved): settled. π/∂_ωε₁ = (πω_pl/2)Z_L with Kozaczuk–Lin's Z_L to O(q²);
   equivalently (πω_p²/2ω_pl)(1 + O(q⁴)).
5. **Phonon branching** 10⁻³–10⁻² is an order-of-magnitude estimate (6.5's vertex-over-denominator estimate), labelled "not computed" in the text. A computation
   (phonon-assisted ε₂ at 15–18 eV, |q| ≤ 2 keV) could be specified in App. E if wanted; 10.10 may list it.
6. **Validity band of Limit 10.3a** (4.2 eV to k_F v_max = 9.5 eV) is a choice of this book, named in the caption: from the upper
   Si absorption peak (4.4: 3.4 and 4.2 eV) to the largest energy deposited at q = k_F.
7. The tables' small-q loss peak (18.9 eV vs measured 16.4 eV) is attributed to the scissor shift raising interband energies,
   by the mechanism of WE 4.10 ("binding pushes up"); not separately verified (a no-scissor run would test it).
8. ω_pl/q is not monotonic all the way to q_c (minimum at 0.989 q_c, 5e-4 below the q_c value); the text says so and uses
   v_th ≃ ω₊(q_c)/q_c.
