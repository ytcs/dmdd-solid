## The reader now knows
- Neutron S^neu per formula unit (N_fu per cell Ω): Tr[P_T S] = 2πN_fu S^neu/((γ_n r_cl)²Ω) (eq:14.4-PT) = Berlin Eq. (26); dictionary
  χ''_ab = 2(1 − e^{−βω})S_ab (Berlin Eq. (B4), same q), e⁴/[8m_e²(2π)³] = r_cl²/4π. Debye–Waller stays in S.
- Thermal transfer: S(T→0) = (1 − e^{−ω/T_d})S(T_d) for free magnons (FDT, T-independent response); residual follows M_s(T). YIG data < 10 K.
- Quenched ion: L = (g−2)S, λ_S = 1 to second order; neutron couples to (g/2)F^mag_g S with F^mag_g = ⟨j0⟩ + (1−2/g)⟨j2⟩; keyresult
  eq:14.4-data with C_sn(q) = [λ_S⟨j0⟩/((g/2)F^mag_g)]²; tensor g and J-multiplet versions; C_sn(0) = 1 (Gd³⁺), 1/9 (Er³⁺), 25 (Sm³⁺).
- Ni²⁺: g ≃ 2.2 → C_sn(0) = 0.83 (λ_S = g−1 would give 1.19); ⟨j2⟩/⟨j0⟩ 0.035 (2.6 keV), 0.15 (5.3 keV); C/C(0) 0.994, 0.973, within 10 %
  to 10 keV; NiO x-ray L/S = 0.34 (DFT+U+SOC 0.36) → g = 2.34, C_sn(0) = 0.73: ~10 % normalization nuisance. Computational connection →
  app:E.20-magnet (g, orbital moments with SOC), app:E.20 (radial integrals).
- Limit Check 14.4a (lim:14.4a, fig:14-limit-neutron): C_sn vs g−2; spin-only reading within 10 % for g−2 ≤ 0.094 at q ≤ 5.3 keV.
- Coverage (Berlin): 120 meV neutrons, p_n = 15.0 keV = momentum of 5.7 MeV DM (its energy 20 eV); 5 meV resolution; detectors 3–60°;
  one-axis rotation; angular average and constant extrapolation to q_1BZ = 987 eV; m < 186 keV entirely inside the first zone; agreement with
  Heisenberg model near 1 MeV; Fe³⁺ form factor ×1/10 for q ≳ 10 keV; phonons ∝ (q/30 keV)²; spin flip with P ∥ q̂ removes them; PSD check.
- Tensors: O4 δ; O6, O10 q̂q̂; O9 and magnetic-dipole/anapole spin–spin part P_T (O9: |c9|²q²P_T/4m_e²). eq:14.4-split. Data fix P_T couplings,
  give a lower bound for O4 (positivity), nothing for O6/O10; velocity tensors (O7, O12–O15) need mixed elements. ALP absorption (eq:14.3-abs)
  is longitudinal and at q ~ 10⁻³m; permeability at q → 0.
- Keyresult (no-go): only the transverse block is measured (≤ 4 of 9 real numbers); magnetic-point-group elements give no new equation
  (Tr[P_T(Rq̂)S(Rq)] invariant; little-group elements preserve the split). Presented as the standard projection rule (Lovesey).
- Fig. 14.4 (fig:14-neutron): geometry and the 3×3 element map.
- Spin-rotation symmetry about m̂ (collinear, no SOC): S = S_∥m̂m̂ + S_⊥(1−m̂m̂) + iS_c ε·m̂; eq:14.4-uniax; polarized data at one q suffice;
  isotropy hypothesis (S_∥, S_⊥ depend on q only via |q|, or unchanged when moments are turned at fixed q, e.g. YIG sphere > 82 mT);
  two-angle inversion eq:14.4-invert. Shortcuts: isotropic ½ (Berlin Eq. (35)); LSWT S_∥ = 0 (Eqs. (36)–(37)), which drops the NiO two-magnon
  continuum (7.3 %).
- WE 14.4 (we:14-paramagnet): c = 0, 1; 5 % errors → σ(S_⊥) 5 %, σ(S_∥) 11 %, L at cos²θ = 1/3: 3.7 %; isotropy test ±14 %; c₂ = 0.25 → 32 %.
- Open problem (stated as physics): no data-only route to q̂·S·q̂ from unpolarized neutrons; routes: isotropy, model fit (normalization
  nuisance, sec:8.8), permeability at q → 0.
