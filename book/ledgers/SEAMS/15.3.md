## The reader now knows
- 𝔩_e = v_F/γ_rel and D_diff = v_F𝔩_e/3 (eq:15.3-le, random-walk derivation); ρ_el𝔩_e = 3π²ħ/(e²k_F²) (eq:15.3-rho), 3.97e-16 Ω m²
  for free-electron Al; 50 nm film → 0.79 μΩ cm, γ_rel = 26.3 meV.
- Three comparisons: q𝔩_e (ballistic vs diffusive), 𝔩_e/ξ_coh (clean vs dirty; ξ_coh = v_F/πΔ_BCS = 2.3 μm), k_F𝔩_e (weak vs strong;
  Ioffe–Regel). tab:15-disorder: Al film vs amorphous WSi.
- Anderson's theorem (recap paragraph): time-reversed exact eigenstates pair; gap equation with the exact-state DOS; Δ, T_c unchanged to
  O(1/k_F𝔩_e); magnetic impurities suppress the gap. Film gap (2Δ = 0.6 meV convention, 1.5) to be used for films.
- Small-q Lindhard R⁰ = −2𝒩_F𝔣_L(ν), 𝔣_L = 1 − (ν/2)ℓ(ν) (eq:15.3-fL); Mermin at q ≪ k_F (eq:15.3-mermin); diffusion pole
  (eq:15.3-diff); keyresult eq:15.3-limits: L_Mer → ωγ_rel/ω_p² = ω/σ_dc (= ε₀ωρ_el in SI) for q𝔩_e ≪ 1, → πωq/(2k_TF²v_F) for
  q𝔩_e ≫ 1, ratio πq𝔩_e/6; within 10 % for q𝔩_e ≤ 0.63 and ≥ 10; 1.43 at q𝔩_e = 1.5.
- Physical picture: Landau damping (share ω/qv_F) vs Joule heating (resistivity).
- Al film at q_min (ω = 0.90 meV): L 24× clean; enhancement for q < 1/𝔩_e = 3.9 eV, i.e. m_χ < 0.75 keV everywhere.
- Drude at finite q: right L for q𝔩 ≪ 1, wrong ε (no diffusion pole); ω+iγ Lindhard: insulator, L = 2× Drude at every q𝔩 ≪ 1.
- Plasmon width (Al Fröhlich fit 0.863 eV, 2101.08263) ≠ transport γ_rel (26 meV for the film); factor 33 overstatement at meV.
- Dirty local superconducting film: ε = 1 + iσ/ω, L = ωσ₁/(σ₁² + σ₂²), L_s/L_n = σ_nσ₁/(σ₁²+σ₂²) (eq:15.3-MB) with Mattis–Bardeen σ
  (15.4 derives): 0.045, 0.235, 0.744, 1.302, 1.259, 1.074 at y = 2.05, 2.2, 2.5, 3, 5, 10; missing weight (π²/2)Δσ_n; mechanism:
  continuity → current (case II) and σ₂ ≫ σ₁ near threshold. Pointer to tab:15-edge (15.2) for intermediate 𝔩_e.
- Thickness: bulk for qd ≫ 1 (correction O(1/qd), surface plasmons ~11 eV); sheet for qd ≪ 1: eq:15.3-sheet,
  ε_sh − 1 = [q_∥d/(ε_a+ε_b)](ε − 1); screening factor up by [(ε_a+ε_b)/q_∥d]²; directional (QROCODILE thin-layer solution).
- WE 15.3 (we:15-film): all numbers of the 50 nm film at ω = 5Δ, q = 0.340 eV; sheet factor 540 in vacuum.
- Film properties a rate needs: ρ_el (→ 𝔩_e), T_c/tunneling gap, d_film with substrate/cover ε, k_F𝔩_e.
- Branch cut and causality: cut |ω| ≥ 2Δ at T = 0; oddness from reality + inversion/TR (not causality); retarded branch (acausal
  branch suppresses Al rate up to 10⁷, 2101.08263); time-ordered = retarded only at ω > 0, T = 0; relaxation must be analytic,
  conserving and dissipative (Mermin yes; ω+iγ no; complex local-field factor gives negative L, 2101.08263 Fig. S4); AB pole of the
  proper response is a pole of ε (zero of 1/ε), no weight in L, enters Re ε up to y ≈ 2.3; Born impurities add no sub-gap absorption at
  T = 0; gap inhomogeneity starts the cut at min 2Δ; thermal e^{−Δ/T}.
- Amorphous WSi: measured R_s, ρ, D, N₀, ξ(0), T_c (Zhang Table I); QROCODILE T_c = 1.73 K → Δ = 0.263 meV (= 2609.06423's WSi gap);
  free gas at 7 eV inconsistent (DOS ×2.3–3.6); 𝔩_e = 0.13–0.41 nm, k_F𝔩_e = 1.8–5.5, γ_rel 2.5–7.7 eV (QROCODILE width 0.7 eV is
  3.6–11× smaller); no small parameter at q = 1–5 keV; at the edge the diffusion pole holds model-independently, L = ε₀ωρ_el =
  (1.2–1.5)e-5 at 2Δ; mean-field BCS fails at k_F𝔩_e of a few; computational connection → app:E.20; open problem stated.

## Open questions / for the editor
1. Overlap with 15.2: 15.2's "Mean free path at the edge" (sec:15.2-disorder, tab:15-edge) quotes the Born-impurity table and names the
   Mattis–Bardeen mechanism. 15.3 now points to it and adds only the local closed form eq:15.3-MB with its computed values (which 15.2's
   30 nm column approaches). The editor may move the MB sentence of 15.2 here or vice versa.
2. Sheet screening (eq:15.3-sheet) and its factor (2/q_∥d)² = 540 for the WE film at q_min: derived in a few lines (density uniform
   across the film, q d ≪ 1, |ε| ≫ 1); no public source states the factor for dark-matter rates (QROCODILE solves the general slab
   numerically). Derivation review requested; it implies that keV-mass rates in thin Al films are far less screened than bulk rates.
3. E.20 prints "k_F𝔩_e ≈ 1" for WSi; the measured transport gives 1.8–5.5 depending on the free-gas parameter used. Align E.20 to
   "a few" (or "≈ 2–5").
4. The superconducting dirty-limit numbers of tab:15-edge come from the validated deferred computation (Born impurities, T = 0, no
   anisotropy, no interaction–disorder corrections). They have no App. D/E home yet beyond the spec of E.20's clean calculation.
5. v_F of Al: 15.3 follows 7.2/E.20 (6.67e-3) with k_F = 1.75 Å⁻¹ (6.4), which implies m* = 1.012 m_e; 6.4's E_F = 11.7 eV gives
   6.77e-3. A book-wide note may be wanted.
6. "QROCODILE uses ω + iγ": their supplement states a Lindhard function with plasmon width Γ = 0.1E_F without the prescription; the text
   says that a width added as ω → ω + iγ, as in 2101.08263 Eq. (S.13) (same authors), is this substitution.
7. The WSi statement "electrons that diffuse slowly repel each other more strongly" (interaction–disorder enhancement) is given without
   citation (no source read).
