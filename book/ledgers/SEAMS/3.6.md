## The reader now knows
- Wannier transform for an isolated band (eq:3.6-wannier1), translation property w_n(r − R), inverse = Bloch sum of 3.2
  (eq:3.6-inverse) via the dual lattice sum Σ_k e^{ik·R} = Nδ_{R,0}; orthonormality (S^ov = 1); dimension Ω^{-1/2}.
- Gauge freedom: phase φ_n(k) changes w; periodic gauge → w(R) is a Fourier coefficient of a periodic function of k; random phases →
  delocalized; smooth → faster than any power; analytic in a strip of half-width 1/ξ_W → e^{−|R|/ξ_W}; exponential localization in
  2D/3D iff Chern invariants vanish (Brouder et al. 2007), true with time-reversal symmetry (Si).
- Composite groups: Si valence degenerate at Γ; projector P(k) smooth; N_W WFs with unitary U(k) (eq:3.6-wannier = eq:A.5-wannier);
  ψ^W, u^W not eigenstates; U periodic; the group's space is spanned exactly.
- Projection gauge U = A^pr (A^pr†A^pr)^{-1/2} (eq:3.6-projection), phases of the code's states cancel; Si valence → 4 bond-centered WFs.
- Spread functional Ω_W (eq:3.6-spread), MLWFs (Foster–Boys); position-operator subtlety: r not periodic on the BvK torus; Blount
  forms derived (eq:3.6-blount): ⟨w_mR|r|w_n0⟩ = iΩ/(2π)³∫e^{ik·R}⟨u_m|∇_k u_n⟩, ⟨r²⟩ = Ω/(2π)³∫‖∇_k u‖²; centre = zone-averaged Berry
  connection, defined mod R; link to Berry-phase polarization (eq:E.4-berry), −2e/Ω × sum of valence centres.
- Discrete forms with overlaps M_mn(k, Δk) and weights λ_Δk (eq:3.6-discrete, MV Eqs. (31)–(32)); branch of ln = discrete ambiguity.
- Split Ω_W = Ω_W^I + Ω̃_W (eq:3.6-split), derived by inserting P + Q; Ω_W^I gauge invariant (depends on P only); minimization of Ω̃_W;
  1D: eigenstates of PxP; 3D: non-commuting PxP, PyP, PzP; stationarity condition (eq:3.6-stationary, MV Eq. (52)).
- Disentanglement: rectangular U(k); Souza subspace selection by minimizing Ω_W^I = spillage (eq:3.6-spillage); frozen window exact;
  Si sp³ WF shown (Fig. 3.11), pointing away from the bonds; bond-pointing set less localized; practice → E.5.
- H^W(R) = ⟨w_m0|H_e|w_nR⟩ = N⁻¹Σ_k e^{−ik·R}[U†EU] (eq:3.6-HWR, derived); H^W(k) = U†EU (eq:3.6-HWk); = tight-binding problem of
  eq:3.2-LCAO with S^ov = 1 and computed hoppings (fulfils 3.2's promise). Computational connection → App. E.5.
- Aliasing: coarse-grid H̃^W(R) = Σ_{L_sc} H^W(R + L_sc) (eq:3.6-alias, derived from the coarse-grid lattice sum).
- Keyresult (eq:3.6-exact): finite range R_max inside the Wigner–Seitz supercell (boundary vectors shared with weights) → exact bands
  at every k. Exponential tails → exponentially small error; MLWFs → shortest range; disentangled bands exact only in the frozen window.
- Si numbers (Figs. 3.11–3.12, PBE): sp³ MLWF (gauge B) 2.02 Å² each, Ω_W = 16.2, Ω_W^I = 14.0 Å² (6³); centre 0.80 Å from its atom, main
  lobe opposite one bond, toward the empty tetrahedral site; |w| decay length 1.57 Å; bond-pointing hybrids (gauge A) are also stationary,
  19.8 Å² (same Ω_W^I) — false-minimum example. Valence bond WFs: Ω_W 7.58 Å² (6³; MV Table I 7.611); interpolation errors 350/120/51 meV
  (4³/6³/8³); H^W along ⟨110⟩: 1.2 eV (3.84 Å), 0.12 eV (7.7 Å), 0.03 eV (11.5 Å), vs 0.02 eV at 5.4 Å. sp³ on 8³: valence ≤ 40 meV,
  conduction below 1.5 eV ≤ 84 meV, worst near the CBM (between grid points) → denser conduction grids (E.5). PBE gap 0.557 eV at
  0.843(2π/a) (= Fig. 3.6 value at a_exp).
- ∇_k H^W(k) analytically; group velocities from its band diagonal (Hellmann–Feynman, eq:3.2-vg); interband v_mn need ⟨w|r|w⟩ too.
- One gauge: general operator O^W = U†OU, k-changing vertices U†(k+q)ρU(k) (eq:E.5-ginterp); mismatch D(k) → gauge DU, unbounded
  real-space tail, O(1) errors between grid points; moduli survive on the grid for nondegenerate bands, lost for degenerate (Si VBM);
  energies show nothing. Second-order Σ_m g_cm ρ_mv needs the same states (A.5 rule restated with its reason; practice in E.5).
- DM density vertex: q is a parameter, not a grid momentum; real-space form per q (E.5).
- WE 3.10 (we:3-w1d): two-site chain, E_a = −E_b = 0.5 eV, t_hop = 2.0, t'_hop = 1.0 eV (illustrative); bands ±1.118…±3.041 eV, gap
  2.236 eV. Two WFs = orbitals; range a; exact for n_k ≥ 3 (1e-15–3e-15 eV), n_k = 2 error 0.24 eV (aliasing of ±a onto the shared

## Open questions / for the editor
1. **Summary of 3.5.** The chapter Summary has a paragraph on 3.5, written from the 3.5 spine key results and the draft s3-5.tex that
   appeared during this work (no digits quoted). The 3.5 writer or the editor should check it against the accepted 3.5.
2. **Notation for the finite-difference vectors.** The literature writes b and w_b; b_i are the reciprocal primitive vectors (3.1) and
   w_n the Wannier function, so the text writes Δk (as E.4 does for Berry strings) and λ_Δk. The overlap is M_mn(k, Δk) with both
   arguments mandatory (M is a mass, entry 24). The literature's J (number of WFs) is N_W (E.5); its N (k points) is n_k (E.1).
3. **Decorated Ω.** Clash entry 29 says no other Ω in Part I; 3.6 needs the parts of the spread: Ω_W^I (gauge-invariant) and Ω̃_W
   (gauge-dependent), both keeping the W. wannier90 prints them as Omega_I, Omega_D, Omega_OD; the text does not use Ω_D, Ω_OD.
4. **Trial orbitals and projection matrix.** The literature's g_n and A_mn clash with the vertex g and the affinity A (3.4); proposed
   φ^tr_n (φ_j family of 3.2) and A^pr(k).
5. **P(k).** The manifold projector P(k) sits beside P_c(k) (conduction projector, 3.4) and the real-space P of the group; the
   argument and the subscript separate them; clash entry 62 (P) could list them.
6. **Second representative calculation.** Figs. 3.11–3.12 are a QE + wannier90 PBE calculation (SG15, 40 Ry, a = 5.431 Å), consistent
   with the code standard; its gap, 0.557 eV, equals E.1's PBE value at the experimental lattice constant.
7. **Orientation of the sp³ MLWFs.** With wannier90's default `Si:sp3` on both atoms the start is mixed (atom 1's hybrids along its bonds,
   atom 2's away from them) and the minimization ends in a symmetry-broken stationary point (2.19/1.81 Å² on 4³). Symmetric starts give
   two stationary points with the same Ω_W^I: along the bonds (19.8 Å², 6³) and away from them (16.2 Å²). The text and Fig. 3.11 use the
   lower one and call the other "a less localized stationary point", not claiming a global minimum. Souza et al. (their silicon section, "sp³ hybrids") report
   the sp³ set without stating the orientation; E.5 says "eight sp³ hybrids, four per atom", which stays true. Both symmetric sets are real (wannier90 Max Im/Re ratio 0.000 for
   function 1 of each), so real-valuedness does not separate them; the text says so. The numbers are printed by fig3_12.py.
8. **Slow convergence of Si interpolation.** Valence errors of 51 meV on 8³ are larger than the folklore "meV on modest grids"; the cause
   (0.12 eV hoppings two lattice vectors away along ⟨110⟩) is stated in the text. Spreads match MV Table I, eigenvalues are symmetric
   to 1e-11 eV over cubic orbits, and an independent nscf 8³ grid confirms the errors of the 4³/6³ interpolation (272/75 meV max), so
   the pipeline is not at fault. E.5's statement that 12³ coarse grids are used for the conduction bands is consistent.
9. **Exponential localization statement.** Attributed to Brouder et al. (2007), not to the RMP; Kohn (1959) and des Cloizeaux are not
   cited (not fetched).
