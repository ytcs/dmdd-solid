## ER-1 Alig–Bloom constant: 5.2 against 5.703 eV^3

### Correct statement
Both values are right. They calibrate different settings:
- **5.2** is the constant of Alig, Bloom and Struck. They fitted it so that their model, which has no plasmon channel, reproduces the measured pair energy of silicon (nielsen2026first Sect. II, Eq. (5)–(6) and the text after them; Sect. V says "specifically fitted to 5.2 so that the method excluding plasmon emission would succeed"). Ramanathan and Kurinsky list it at **300 K** (Table I, "A 5.2 eV² b 300 K 2–5 eV e− [19]", footnote b: "compared to the value obtained by [18]"). They confirm that it reproduces the room-temperature pair energies of Table I (Sect. IV A, Eq. (12): ε_eh = 1.7E_g + 0.084A + 1.3).
- **5.703 eV^3** is the book's own calibration at **123 K**, E_g = 1.160 eV. It uses the moment recursions of Sect. 18.2, θ_ii = 1, holes cut at 12 eV and no plasmon channel, and it is fitted to ε_eh = 3.749 eV (Rodrigues et al.). Evidence: figscripts/ch18/si_cascade18.log ("continuum calibration on eps_eh = 3.749 eV: A_AB = 5.7032 eV^3") and table18_4.log ("F_eh = 0.11419 … model below by 2.40 sigma").

The difference between the two constants comes from the temperature of the datum. The rerun (`ab52.out`) gives:

| A_AB | E_g (eV) | ε_eh (eV) | F_eh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.703 | 1.160 | 3.7490 | 0.11419 |
| 5.2 | 1.160 | 3.7079 | 0.11348 |
| 5.2 | 1.12 (300 K) | 3.6399 | 0.11461 |
| 5.2 | 1.17 | 3.7249 | 0.11321 |

At the 300 K gap, 5.2 gives 3.64 eV in the book's cascade. That lies inside the measured 300 K values of 3.62–3.66 eV (Ramanathan Table I). Ramanathan's Eq. (12) also gives 3.64 eV at E_g = 1.12 eV and A = 5.2.

The 3.71 eV / F = 0.115 that E-13 printed before the Vol III E2E pass were Ramanathan's Eq. (12)–(13) at 5.2 and 1.160 eV, which give 3.709 eV and 0.1153.

Points checked:
- **Units.** Eq. (8) is A × eV^{1/2}/eV^{7/2}, so a dimensionless ratio needs A in eV^3. E-13's note about the source's "eV²" is correct.
- **"10 %" (s18-4).** 5.703/5.2 = 1.097, which is a correct rounding. No change.
- **Attribution.** Both citations hold: nielsen2026first Sect. II for the fit by Alig et al., and ramanathan2020ionization Table I for the value.
- **Stale values.** No "0.115" or "two standard deviations" in the cascade context survives anywhere. The grep hits are unrelated: s11-5, s13-9, s21-3, the full-band envelope [0.101, 0.115], s1-5 and s8-8.
- **Two citation slips in the E-13 sentence.** Ramanathan has no "Table 1"; the table is Table I. The hot-electron validation (Chang et al.) is in Sect. III (Data, item 1), not Sect. II.

## ER-9 Stillinger–Weber silicon threshold: 17.5 eV against 18 eV

### Correct statement
These are the same quantity at two roundings. Kadribasic et al. (1703.05371, text after Fig. 1) give: "The Ge threshold ranges from 12.5 eV to 63.5 eV whereas that for Si ranges from 17.5 eV to 63.5 eV". Their values come from eight runs averaged on the threshold surface, with energies stepped by 1 eV.

Sassi et al. (2206.06772, Table II) give E_t,min = 18 eV for Si and 13 eV for Ge, in whole eV. Their Sect. II A says the C, Si and Ge simulations "were done previously using the PARCAS code". Their ref. [20] is Kadribasic et al., and the Si potential (SW) and Ge potential (modified SW) are the same as Kadribasic's. Both of Sassi's minima are Kadribasic's half-eV values rounded up, so the sources do not disagree.

## ER-23 "Dense tracks" pointer in E-23

- **First sentence, correct as printed.** "The cascade of \cref{sec:18.1} treats the pairs of one event as independent, each subtree evolving alone" matches s18-1 (the second hypothesis of the branching process: "the carriers of one cascade evolve independently, with no Pauli blocking and no Coulomb interaction among them"). s18-1 also gives the expansion parameter E-23 uses: "the pair density times $a_x^3$ is 4.7 at \SI{1}{keV}". It ends "for keV deposits it is an assumption whose error has not been estimated". The E-23 calculation is what estimates that error.
- **sec:18.5.** Dense tracks appear in sec:18.5-excitons: "When excitons form from carriers of different pairs, as in a dense track, the loss is not independent from pair to pair, … and the variance acquires a part that grows faster than $\langle N_{\mathrm{eh}}\rangle$". That is the change of F_NR. They also appear in sec:18.5-kinetics, as two-body processes in dense tracks. Neither section gives a number.

So "which sec:18.5, sec:19.5 need" is loose. The ratio and the change of F_NR answer s18-1 (the unestimated error) and sec:18.5-excitons (the excess variance). sec:19.5-heat needs the two-body loss rates, which the Auger channel of the same runs supplies.
