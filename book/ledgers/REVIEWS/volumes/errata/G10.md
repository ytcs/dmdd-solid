#### ER-42 (tex/ch32/E-23.tex:23, app:E.23, created-phonon spectrum)
Verdict: AMEND (a comparand is needed; the reviewer's "excess lattice energy" is ambiguous, and the specification names the heat).
Evidence: the sentence is unchanged from base 60cc72c, so the gap is in the original. Its source is the specification returns/R14/WP5-AUD.md:141, "Record the anharmonic residual ℋ_MD − ΣE_s (check: ≤ 10% at t_pr)". There ΣE_s is the zero-point-subtracted sum Σ_s(E_s − E_s^(0)) (l. 138–140), and ℋ_MD is the heat of the classical run, "its heat ℋ_MD equals ℋ_ph" (l. 52), including the energy absorbed by the boundary thermostat (l. 99). The 10 % is therefore a fraction of the heat. In E-23 that quantity is $E_{\mathrm{heat}}$, "all of the phonon energy" (E-23:17), separated from $E_{\mathrm{st}}$ by the quench at $t_{\mathrm{pr}}$ (E-23:21). "The energy of the excess lattice state" would be the wrong reference: the paragraph subtracts the initial projection after projecting, so that energy still carries the zero-point energy, 126 eV in a 4096-atom cell (E-23:21), and below about 100 eV it would loosen the test by a large factor. referees/R14/REF-WP5-AUD-A.md:89 calls the 10 % level itself unsourced. That is a separate point; this entry concerns only the missing comparand. "anharmonic residual" occurs nowhere else in tex/.
- old: `the anharmonic residual, must stay below \SI{10}{\percent}.`
- new: `the anharmonic residual, must stay below \SI{10}{\percent} of the heat $E_{\mathrm{heat}}$.`

#### ER-43 (tex/ch32/E-25.tex:55, app:E.25, two-bias inversion)
Verdict: AMEND (the reviewer's correction is right; the wording keeps the name "the ionized fraction" with its symbol, uses one name for the collection offset, and states the measurement as s19-5 does).
Evidence: s19-5:101 defines $f_{\mathrm{ion}} = N^{\mathrm{NR}}_{\mathrm{ind}}/N^{\mathrm{ER}}_{\mathrm{ind}}$ as a ratio of bias works, which "is smaller if the carriers of the dense track are trapped more often"; s20-1:58 and s20-1:88 show that trapping and the collected fraction depend on the field, and that trapping lowers $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$. s19-5:319 lists the hypotheses of the inversion: the collection ratio does not change between the biases; "The ionized fraction must be measured at both biases with the charge channel, since trapping depends on the field"; the offset and the missing energies are the same at both biases; same $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and known $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eh}}$. It then says "A third bias overdetermines \cref{eq:19.5-linear} and tests the first three hypotheses". The inversion does not need a bias-independent $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$. In eq:19.5-linear (s19-5:314) a measured $f_{\mathrm{ion},i}$ enters only the known coefficient $g_{\mathrm{Luke},i}(Y_i - f_{\mathrm{ion}})$ and the right-hand side, while the unknowns $Y_{\mathrm{ph}}(0) = A/B$ and $\mathfrak{r}' = \mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}/B$ (D-45:45) contain no $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$. So the system stays linear and solvable. The statement in s19-5 is therefore the right one. E-25's "do not change with bias" adds an assumption the inversion does not need and that the field dependence of trapping makes false. E-25's base text (60cc72c) already had $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ in the bias-independent list. $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ occurs nowhere else in ch32 or ch33.
- old: `assumes that $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, the ionized fraction $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ and the collection offset do not change with bias and that the reconstruction keeps its pair energy; the run computes the residual from the bias dependence of $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, and a third bias tests whether $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ and the collection offset are independent of bias.`
- new: `assumes that $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$ and the collection offset do not change with bias, that the ionized fraction $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ is measured at both biases with the charge channel, and that the reconstruction keeps its pair energy; the run computes the residual from the bias dependence of $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, and a third bias tests the assumptions on $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ and the collection offset.`
For the lead (not a correction of this entry): eq:19.5-linear (s19-5:314), its solution at D-45:57 and the determinant at s19-5:317 print $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ without the bias index. Taken literally, they use one value at both biases, which conflicts mildly with s19-5:319. An index $f_{\mathrm{ion},i}$ there would make the text consistent; it needs its own erratum entry.

#### Note on E-18 (tex/ch31/E-18.tex:45–55, app:E.18, couplings not proportional to mass)
Verdict: an error that needs a correction. It is not a correct simplification.
Evidence: by eq:12.6-mass (s12-6:104) the mean coupling has the amplitude $-\tilde c\,m_e\langle f|n_e(\vect{k})|i\rangle$. By s12-6:121 and E-18:45 the amplitude of $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ is $\sum_{\vect{G}}b_{\vect{G}}\langle f|n_e(\vect{k}+\vect{G})|i\rangle$ with $b_{\vect{G}} \propto [\vect{k}\cdot(\vect{k}+\vect{G})]\,\Xi_{\vect{G}}(\vect{k})$. Both amplitudes lead to the same final electronic states, so the rate is $|a_0|^2S_{00} + 2\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{\vect{G}}a_0b^*_{\vect{G}}S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}} + \sum_{\vect{G}\vect{G}'}b_{\vect{G}}b^*_{\vect{G}'}S^{ee}_{\vect{G}\vect{G}'}$, with $a_0 = -\tilde c\,m_e$. \Cref{eq:E.18-X} is only the last term, quadratic in $\delta\tilde c$. The middle term is linear in $\delta\tilde c$, and nothing makes it vanish: $\Xi_{\vect{0}}$ is weighted by $Z_\kappa$, not by $M_\kappa$, so it is not zero when $\tilde c$ is the mean of $c_\kappa/M_\kappa$; and $S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}} \neq 0$ once local fields are kept. Counting orders, $|b|/|a_0| \sim (\delta\tilde c/\tilde c)\,\omega_p^2/\omega^2$, so the cross term exceeds \cref{eq:E.18-X} by about $(\tilde c/\delta\tilde c)\,\omega^2/\omega_p^2$. For the proton coupling in GaAs ($\delta\tilde c/\tilde c \approx 0.5$ %, s12-6:121) near the plasmon that is a factor of order $10^2$. So "the part of the rate that $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ adds" is false for \cref{eq:E.18-X}. The $\vect{G} = 0$ pieces of both amplitudes multiply $n_e(\vect{k})$, so they change only the coefficient of the loss-function rate \cref{eq:12.6-absrate}. The following base sentence, that the effect "lies in the off-diagonal elements", is correct for everything beyond that coefficient. The new sentences make that reading explicit. The equation itself and the acceptance tests of E-18:57 (2 % convergence of Γ, the vanishing (200) channel, the reduction for $c_\kappa \propto M_\kappa$) stay valid for the quadratic part. The base 60cc72c ("remainder") had the same gap.
1. - old: `The part of the rate that $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ adds is then`
   - new: `The part of the rate quadratic in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ is then`
2. - old: `and every Umklapp term is $O(k)$, like the $\vect{G} = 0$ term.`
   - new: `and every Umklapp term is $O(k)$, like the $\vect{G} = 0$ term. The mean coupling $\tilde c$ has the amplitude $-\tilde c\,m_e\langle f|n_e(\vect{k})|i\rangle$ of \cref{eq:12.6-mass}. Interference between this amplitude and the amplitude of $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ adds to the rate a cross term linear in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$, with the elements $S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}}$ and the weights $[\vect{k}\cdot(\vect{k}+\vect{G})]\,\Xi^*_{\vect{G}}(\vect{k})$. The $\vect{G} = 0$ terms of the two amplitudes both multiply $n_e(\vect{k})$ and change only the coefficient of the loss-function rate \cref{eq:12.6-absrate}.`
For the lead (pointers, not corrections, since the entry names only E-18): s12-6:121 says "The mean part is \cref{eq:12.6-absrate}. The remainder …", and the row "Umklapp remainder (\cref{eq:E.18-X})" of tab:12-nuisances (s12-6:151) has the same gap between amplitude and rate. The words "The whole effect" in the base sentence at E-18:55 are loose for the $\vect{G} = \vect{G}' = 0$ term. With the new sentence before it, they read as "beyond the coefficient of the loss-function rate", and they stay.
