## ER-7 (VALUE) Optical-phonon energy of silicon: 60 meV against 64 meV

**Correct statement.** These are two quantities and both values are right. 64 meV (64.1 meV, 15.5 THz) is the zone-center optical phonon of silicon. 60 meV is the Einstein energy ω₀ that Kahn and Lin choose for their single-site model, "a typical phonon energy for silicon", averaged over the branches. The 60 meV in the readout table tab:1-quanta labels the optical phonon, so it is the wrong one of the two there and should read 64 meV. lim:1.3a keeps 60 meV and gets one clause saying what that value is.

**Evidence.**
- Nilsson and Nelin (nilsson1972study), cited in s1-3:125, s5-1:3 and s5-2:115, give 15.5 THz at Γ. h × 15.5 THz = 4.1357e-15 eV s × 15.5e12 s⁻¹ = 0.0641 eV. The book uses this value everywhere else: s1-3:125, the s1-3:91 caption, s1-5:40 ("optical phonons of about 64 meV"), s2-5:3, s5-1:3 (64.1 meV), s5-2:3, ch07.tex:4, s9-3:28.
- Kahn and Lin, kahn2022searches = book/sources/1.3/2108.03239.txt. In the text around Eq. (80), at l.1998–2003 of the extract: "if we take ω0 = 60 meV, a typical phonon energy for silicon ... The motivation for using ω0 = 60 meV is that if a single nucleus is struck with a large momentum transfer, the motion will have a large overlap with the phonon modes at the edges of the 1BZ, where the ions have more random motions and energies ∼ 60 meV." At l.1640: "a typical phonon energy ω0 that will be some average over acoustic and optical phonons".
- figscripts/ch01/limit1_nuclear.py:23 has `OMEGA0 = 0.060  # eV [KL, below Eq. (80)]`. The figure of lim:1.3a is therefore correct at 60 meV. Nothing in the table feeds a computation.

**Changes.**

## ER-16 (DERIVATION) Connected off-sector term in aluminum: is "about 0.2 eV" h_off or its square?

**Correct statement.** 0.2 eV is the size of the connected part of h_off, an energy. The largest eigenvalue of the zeroth-moment matrix, which bounds its square, is about 0.03 eV². Read as the eigenvalue, 0.2 would need units of eV² and would put h_off at 0.45 eV, which the electron–phonon coupling of aluminum does not give. The bin-width condition of s8-6, bins wide compared with h_off, therefore means bins wide compared with about 0.2 eV.

**Evidence.** There is no citation at either location, so this is an independent estimate from the book's own α²F of aluminum (sec:6.4, fig:6-a2F, figscripts/ch06/fig6_6.log).
- Integrate eq:6.4-a2F over ω. This gives ∫α²F dω = ⟨N⁻¹ Σ_{Qmν} |g_{mnν}|² δ(ξ_{m,k+Q})⟩_FS.
- Take |g|² as constant over the final band. Then N⁻¹ Σ_Q δ(ξ) = 𝒩_F Ω.
- The T = 0 zeroth moment of eq:6.3-ImFM, the diagonal entry of the matrix with one final band and 2n + 1 → 1, is then M₀ ≈ ∫α²F dω / 𝒩_F Ω.
- fig6_6.log gives `int a2F d omega = 5.39 meV` and `N_F Omega (per spin, per cell) = 0.1931 /eV`. Script er16_hoff_al.py:
  `fig6_6 model: M0 = 0.0279 eV^2, sqrt(M0) = 0.167 eV`; `same int a2F, Savrasov N_F Omega: M0 = 0.0267 eV^2, sqrt(M0) = 0.163 eV`; `if 0.2 eV were the eigenvalue (eV^2): h_off = sqrt(0.2) = 0.447 eV`.
- The electron–electron part, which the text says adds to the moment, is not in this estimate. It raises the value, and "about 0.2 eV" for the square root is consistent with 0.17 eV plus that part.
- What the evidence does not settle: the estimate rests on constant |g|² and one final band. It fixes the order and the units, not the second digit. The wording below says "about".

   - new: `and in aluminum that eigenvalue is about \SI{0.03}{eV^2}, so the connected part is about \SI{0.2}{eV} (from $\int\dd\omega\,\alpha^2F/\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{F}}\Omega$ of \cref{fig:6-a2F}).`

## ER-26 (TEXT) Observation time for a classical field: s8-2 against D-25

**Correct statement.** The two passages agree. They state two different conditions. Proposal: **no change.**

**Derivation.**
- Take a field with spectral density S_φ(ω) of width Δω_φ = 1/t_coh, acting on a target whose response has width Γ. The ensemble-averaged transition probability in an observation time T is P(T) = ∫dω S_φ(ω) ∫dω_f D(ω_f) sin²[(ω − ω_f)T/2]/[(ω − ω_f)/2]².
- This becomes 2πT times the overlap, the golden rule, once T is long compared with the inverse width of the convolution of S_φ and D. That width is of order max(1/t_coh, Γ). The condition is therefore T ≫ min(t_coh, 1/Γ), the "shorter of" in s8-2:81.
- A single realization has, in addition, cross terms φ_k φ*_k′ with k ≠ k′, which the ensemble average removes by construction. In one record they average out in time only for T ≫ 1/Δω_φ = t_coh. That is D-25:86 ("a single realization does the same in time, once the observation time exceeds the inverse spread of the frequencies ω_k, which is the coherence time"), and the second clause of s8-2:81 ("a single record reproduces it only when it is also long compared with t_coh").
- When T is below both t_coh and 1/Γ, neither condition holds and the amplitude grows coherently. This is the last sentence of s8-2:81.

**Evidence that the current text already matches.** s8-2:81: "The ensemble average needs an observation time long compared with the shorter of the coherence time ... and the inverse width of the target's response; a single record reproduces it only when it is also long compared with $t_{\mathrm{coh}}$." D-25:86 treats only the single-realization cross terms. No change.
