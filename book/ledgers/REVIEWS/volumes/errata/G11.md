#### ER-45 (tex/ch12/s12-6.tex:121, sec:12.6, and the last row of tab:12-nuisances, s12-6:151)
Verdict: an error, the same gap as ER-44. It needs a correction at both locations.

Evidence. s12-6:121 splits $c_\kappa/M_\kappa = \tilde c + \delta\tilde c_\kappa$. The mean part has the amplitude $a_0\langle f|n_e(\vect{k})|i\rangle$ with $a_0 = -\tilde c\,m_e$ (eq:12.6-mass). The remainder has the amplitude $\sum_{\vect{G}}b_{\vect{G}}\langle f|n_e(\vect{k}+\vect{G})|i\rangle$, with $b_{\vect{G}} \propto [\vect{k}\cdot(\vect{k}+\vect{G})]\,\Xi_{\vect{G}}(\vect{k})$ (E-18:45–51). Both amplitudes lead to the same final states, so the rate is $|a_0|^2S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{0}} + 2\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{\vect{G}}a_0b^*_{\vect{G}}S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}} + \sum_{\vect{G}\vect{G}'}b_{\vect{G}}b^*_{\vect{G}'}S^{ee}_{\vect{G}\vect{G}'}$. I redid this independently, and it agrees with G10 "Note on E-18". eq:E.18-X is the third term only. s12-6:121 ties the remainder to that equation ("(\cref{eq:E.18-X})"). The table gives the size of the row as "Umklapp remainder (\cref{eq:E.18-X})". Neither location shows the middle term, which is linear in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$. Its ratio to eq:E.18-X is of order $|a_0|/|b| \sim (\tilde c/\delta\tilde c)\,\omega^2/\omega_p^2$ (G10 count), so the middle term dominates wherever $(\delta\tilde c/\tilde c)\,\omega_p^2/\omega^2 \ll 1$. That covers the plasmon region for the proton coupling; near the gap it is not automatic. A $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ common to all species reduces exactly through eq:12.6-mass to $-\delta\tilde c\,m_e\langle f|n_e(\vect{k})|i\rangle$, so the large single terms of order $\omega_p^2/\omega^2$ cancel for the common part. Only the species difference of $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ escapes the cancellation, and the per-term count is an upper estimate.
- The statement "the remainder is negligible" for nucleon number in GaAs (s12-6:121) is left unchanged, but this count does not settle it. The species half-difference is $\delta\tilde c/\tilde c \approx 1.3\times10^{-5}$: half of the $2.6\times10^{-5}$ per u, over $A/M \approx 0.99$ per u. The per-term upper estimate of the cross term relative to the mean rate is $(\delta\tilde c/\tilde c)\,\omega_p^2/\omega^2$. At the plasmon that is about $10^{-5}$. At \SI{1.5}{eV} it is $10^{-3}$ with the valence $\omega_p \approx \SI{16}{eV}$ and $10^{-2}$ with the all-electron $\omega_p \approx \SI{44}{eV}$, since $\Xi_{\vect{G}}$ carries the full $Z_\kappa$. The cancellation of the common part makes the true value smaller, but no calculation sizes it. No number moves.
- The open-problems sentence (s12-6:160, "needs the off-diagonal response $S^{ee}_{\vect{G}\vect{G}'}$ at vanishing reduced momentum") already covers $S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}}$ as the $\vect{G} = 0$ row of that matrix. It needs no change.
- The new wording mirrors the ER-44 draft ("cross term linear in", "the part of the rate quadratic in"). If Stage 3 amends ER-44's terms, ER-45 should follow.

1. s12-6:121. The new sentence goes after "These are nonadiabatic matrix elements …" and not before it, so that "These" keeps its antecedent.
   - old: `the response of the electrons to the force of one sublattice, which no optical measurement provides.`
   - new: `the response of the electrons to the force of one sublattice, which no optical measurement provides. Interference between the remainder and the amplitude \cref{eq:12.6-mass} of the mean part adds to the rate a cross term linear in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$, through the elements $S^{ee}_{\vect{0}\vect{G}}$; \cref{eq:E.18-X} is the part of the rate quadratic in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$.`
2. tab:12-nuisances. The basis stays "named", because no size of the cross term has been computed.
   - old: `Couplings not proportional to mass & normali\-zation & Umklapp remainder (\cref{eq:E.18-X}) & named`
   - new: `Couplings not proportional to mass & normali\-zation & Umklapp remainder: cross term with the mean part, linear in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$, and the part quadratic in $\delta\tilde c_\kappa$ (\cref{eq:E.18-X}) & named`

#### ER-47 (tex/ch19/s19-5.tex:319, sec:19.5, hypotheses of the two-bias inversion)
Verdict: an error. Hypotheses 2 and 3 contradict each other as written. ER-47 comes before ER-46 here because the ER-46 verdict depends on how ER-47 is resolved.

Evidence. eq:19.5-linear holds with one unknown $Y_{\mathrm{ph}}(0) = A/B$ at both biases only if $A = (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{coll}})(1 - d_{\mathrm{NR}})$ and $B = 1 - d_{\mathrm{ER}}$ are the same at both biases (D-45:36–45; hypothesis 3). The book's own model ties $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ to $A$ in two ways:
- The gap part of the offset carries the factor $1 - f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ (eq:19.5-dgap, s19-5:169–171).
- $E_{\mathrm{miss}}$ holds the trapped carriers and their electrostatic energy (s19-5:97). s20-1:88 adds that a trapped carrier keeps its share of the gap energy as missing energy, and that trapping lowers $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ below $Y_q$.

So field-dependent trapping, which is the reason hypothesis 2 gives for measuring $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ at both biases, changes $\delta_{\mathrm{coll}}$ and $d_{\mathrm{NR}}$ and violates hypothesis 3. The only exception would be $\rho_g = 1$ together with no change in $E_{\mathrm{miss}}$, and the text never assumes that. Conversely, hypothesis 3 requires both biases to "collect every pair within the window". With the electron recoil fully collected (s19-5:101), that gives $f_{\mathrm{ion}} = Y_q$ at both biases (s19-5:101, "It equals the ionization yield $Y_q$ … when both recoils are collected alike"), so $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ cannot change with bias if $Y_q$ itself does not depend on the field (s20-1 excludes recombination inside a dense track from its drift model). The verdict does not rest on that point. The eq:19.5-dgap argument decides it alone: any change of $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ between the biases moves $\delta_{\mathrm{coll}}$ unless $\rho_g = 1$, and the proposed text uses only that argument.

The text shows the field dependence where collection is incomplete. The ionization of nuclear recoils is collected in full at the \SI{20}{keV} calibration point (s19-5:293), but only about \SI{75}{\percent} of the parameterized yield is collected at \SI{100}{keV} and \SI{4}{V/cm} (s19-5:309). A field-dependent $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ is therefore real where full collection fails, and there the inversion fails too.

The consistent statement is that $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ must be the same at both biases, and the charge channel at each bias tests that. This is the job the existing sentence on $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ can do honestly. The rewrite stays in place as the second hypothesis, so "A third bias … tests the first three hypotheses" keeps its count and its referents (collection ratio, ionized fraction, offset and missing energies). The next base sentence, "The offset and the missing energies must also be the same at both biases, so both biases must collect every pair within the window", now follows from the new text without change. No number moves: bal19_5.py section 5 uses one $f_{\mathrm{ion}} = y_C(\SI{20}{keV})$ at 4 and \SI{40}{V}.
- old: `The ionized fraction must be measured at both biases with the charge channel, since trapping depends on the field.`
- new: `The ionized fraction must be the same at both biases, and the charge channel measures $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ at each bias. Trapping depends on the field. A trapped carrier lowers $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ and changes the gap part of the offset through the factor $1 - f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ of \cref{eq:19.5-dgap}, and the balance law counts the gap energy that the trapped carrier keeps as missing energy.`

binding.py flags:
- REL[offset] and REL[missing]: base names. "the offset" is the collection offset in the base text of the same paragraph, and "missing energy" is $E_{\mathrm{miss}}$ of the balance law.
- PROP[that]: a restrictive relative.
"$f_{\mathrm{ion}}$" is repeated in place of "it", which had five candidates. "the trapped carrier" repeats "A trapped carrier" in place of "its", which had eight candidates.

For the lead, not an edit, since E-25 is outside this entry. The applied ER-43 text in tex/ch32/E-25.tex:55 says "that the ionized fraction $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ is measured at both biases with the charge channel" and "a third bias tests the assumptions on $\mathfrak{r}_{\mathrm{coll}}$, $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ and the collection offset". Both statements stay true word for word under the new s19-5 text. They are weaker than s19-5, since they do not say that $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ must agree between the biases, but they contradict nothing.

#### ER-46 (eq:19.5-linear at s19-5:314; the determinant at s19-5:317; the system and its inversion in D-45:57–67)
Verdict: not an error once ER-47 is corrected as proposed. No change to the equations. Stage 3 should judge ER-46 and ER-47 together.

Evidence:
- eq:19.5-linear and the D-45 system leave $Y_{\mathrm{ph}}(0)$ and $\mathfrak{r}'$ without a bias index. These symbols state the hypotheses that the two quantities are the same at both biases (s19-5:319, hypotheses 1 and 3).
- Under the corrected hypothesis 2, $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ has the same status, so the unindexed $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ is the consistent notation.
- An index on $f_{\mathrm{ion}}$ alone would not repair anything. If $f_{\mathrm{ion},1} \neq f_{\mathrm{ion},2}$ because of trapping, then $A$ differs between the biases (ER-47), $Y_{\mathrm{ph}}(0)$ would need an index too, and the system would have three unknowns in two equations.
- The worry raised in G10 ("Taken literally, they use one value at both biases") is answered by the new hypothesis text: one value at both biases is what the hypotheses require.

Conditional list, for use only if Stage 3 rejects the ER-47 resolution and wants measured per-bias values $f_{\mathrm{ion},i}$ in the equations. Notation would follow $g_{\mathrm{Luke},i}$, $g_{\mathrm{cal},i}$, written `f_{\mathrm{ion},i}`. Every place that changes:
- s19-5:314, eq:19.5-linear: both occurrences become $f_{\mathrm{ion},i}$.
- s19-5:317, the determinant sentence. The condition would read "When the two measured ratios and the two ionized fractions are equal". The high-bias limit $f_{\mathrm{ion}} + [\dots]$ in the same paragraph is a single-bias statement and keeps its symbol.

- D-45:57: "with gains $g_{\mathrm{Luke},i}$, $g_{\mathrm{cal},i}$ and measured ratios $Y_i$" would gain "and ionized fractions $f_{\mathrm{ion},i}$". The matrix and the right-hand side (D-45:59, 61) change in four places: $f_{\mathrm{ion},1}$ in row 1, $f_{\mathrm{ion},2}$ in row 2.
