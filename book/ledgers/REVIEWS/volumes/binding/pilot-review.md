## Three calibration pairs

| Kind | Old | New / fix | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAD (meaning loss) | "…a state that conserves crystal momentum but not energy … a final state that conserves both" | "…an intermediate state with the same crystal momentum and a different energy … with the energy and the crystal momentum of the initial state plus the photon" | Rebinding the negated pair into "same / different" dropped its argument, and the physics claim (energy not conserved) went with it. | GOOD | "the decaying state has gone somewhere, and the golden-rule rate fixes both how fast and how sharply in energy" | "the probability that leaves the decaying state ends in the continuum, and the golden-rule rate fixes both the speed of the decay and the width of the line" | The vague proposition and the elliptical how-clauses become named noun phrases, so "both" picks from one coordinated noun phrase. Nothing is lost. |
| BAD (over-repair) | "The principal-value part collects the off-shell intermediate states, and it is real: it shifts energies…" | "…and is real; the principal-value part shifts energies…" | The old "it" was the subject of the previous clause and the only candidate, which pattern 27 allows. Repeating the noun here makes the prose robotic. Repeat a noun only when the pronoun has two candidates. |
