# R18 / DC-54: recast of the 2401.11971 CRDM limits with the exact density row (1 − ω²/Q²)² and the transverse row

Spec: returns/R16/CW4.md §18 with CW4-errata A4 (authoritative) and OC-7. Sources read in full text from the arXiv TeX: 2401.11971 main.tex and supp_reply.tex, 2608.20715 (generalised ELF, its Eq. numbered there as the substitution for Im[−1/ε_L]), 1801.04059 (LIS fit), 2004.10709 (P(Z|ω) table, its Supplementary/p100K.dat). ELF tables: DarkELF repository tongylin/DarkELF (Si_gpaw_withLFE.dat, Si_gpaw_noLFE.dat, Ge_gpaw_withLFE.dat). Code: code/DC-54/. Status: CLAIMED.

**Verdict.** The flux and the heavy-mediator spectrum are reproduced. The light-mediator spectrum is **not** reproduced to 5 %, so no corrected light-mediator limit curve is presented as a result. For the heavy mediator R_URF/R_src = 0.9999 [CALC]. For the light mediator the diagnostic is that (1 − ω²/Q²)² is negligible above m_χ ≈ 5 keV but removes most of the rate at m_χ ≈ 1 keV, because there Eq. (5) has a real pole at Q → ω that dominates the rate in the T range of their Fig. 2.

## 1. Quantity
R_URF/R_src per target and flux, with

  dR/dω = (1/ρ_T)(σ̄/2πe²μ²)∫Q³dQ∫dE_χ (dΦ/dT)[(2E_χ − ω)² − Q²]/(4p_χ²) |F_DM(Q²−ω²)|² 𝒮(Q,ω),

which is their (S.14) (I re-derived it from Eq. (4), (5), (6) line by line: the 2π, the Jacobian and the final 1/4v²E² all stand). 𝒮 = Im[−1/ε_L] is the source. The URF density row multiplies by (1 − ω²/Q²)² (CW4 (18.1)). The transverse row adds

  (1 − ω²/Q²)²·(K_T/K)·Im[−Q²/(ω²ε_T − Q²)],  K_T/K → v_χ² − ω²/Q² classically.

*Normalisation of the transverse row [D].* Screening the transverse electron current by the transverse photon gives Im R_T^{scr} = −((Q²−ω²)²/e²)Im[1/(Q²−ω²ε_T)]. So the transverse row carries the same factor F_DM²(1 − ω²/Q²)² as the density row for any m_V, and for m_V → 0 reduces to the 2608.20715 substitution quoted in CW4 §18. The spin-½ contraction is tr_T L = [4p_T² + 2(Q²−ω²)]/(4EE′), with p_T² from exact kinematics; I report both this ("Dirac") and the classical form. They agree to < 10⁻⁴ in every Z-weighted rate.

ε_T(Q,ω) = ε_L^{data}(0,ω) + [ε_T^{Lind}(Q,ω) − (1 − ω_p²/ω²)], with Im ε_T^{Lind} = e²[(k_F²−a²)₊² − (k_F²−b²)₊²]/(16πm_eQω²), a,b = m_eω/Q ∓ Q/2, and Re by Kramers–Kronig (ω_p = 16.6 eV Si, 16 eV Ge; code/DC-54/epsT.py).

## 2. Method
For each (ω, T) the Q integral runs over the exact range [|p − p′|, p + p′], 160 Gauss nodes in ln(Q − Q_min), with Q² − ω² evaluated without cancellation at the edge: (p−p′)² − ω² = 2m²ω²/(EE′ + pp′ − m²). This matters: a plain log Q grid gave 10⁴ for the same integral in my first attempt, which was a rounding artefact, not physics. Convergence: nodes 160 → 320 changes src by 2×10⁻⁷; T grid 420 → 840 changes src by 0.3 % and the density row by 3.8 %, which is the error on ratios below. ELF: linear interpolation in (ω, k), ε₁ = 1, ε₂ = 0 off the grid, as darkelf/epsilon.py. Published curves are vector paths from the arXiv source PDFs. Si only for the limits (the source has no Ge limit).

**Why the 1 keV ratio is small.** Eq. (5) with F_DM at Q² − ω² has a pole at Q = ω. Relativistic DM reaches it, Q_min − ω ≈ ω/2γ². Each T contributes ~ γ²/2 to ∫Q³dQ/(Q²−ω²)², so the rate grows with T_max. Measured at 1 keV (T ≥ 10⁻⁷ GeV, Q ≤ 5 keV), ∫dR/dω relative to Fig. 3:

| T_max (GeV) | 10⁻⁴ | 10⁻³ | 10⁻² | 0.1 | 0.3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| src/pub | 0.22 | 0.24 | 0.33 | 0.69 | 1.02 | 1.40 |
| R_den/R_src | 0.90 | 0.83 | 0.59 | 0.28 | 0.19 | 0.14 |

So at 1 keV the source's formula has no converged T_max: its rate is set by the end of the flux table, and the published level is reached only with the pole included. The URF row has no pole (Coulombic, (q₀²/Q²)²), so the correction is not a small percentage there. This resolves the "UNRESOLVED" of A4 qualitatively: **the sign and mass range of the impact are determined; the size at 1 keV is not**, because the light curve is not reproduced.

**Low-T flux.** With no lower T cut, the density rate at 1 keV rises ×13 in Z = 4–6 (T ≈ 20–100 eV, below the Fig. 2 axis): the URF σ̄ would be 1.6×10⁻³³, ×3.5 *stronger* than the published-window value, opposite to the pole. That flux needs the electron LIS below 2 MeV, outside the fit's stated range, so I give it as a named uncertainty, not a number to quote. Whether the source's T range really stops at 10⁻⁷ GeV is an inference.

**Transverse row.** Si, Z = 4–6: +0.07 % (1 keV light), ≤ 10⁻⁴ otherwise. Differentially it is not small far below the plasmon: at 1 keV light, +3 % at ω = 8 eV, ×2.7 at ω = 4 eV, but ω = 4 eV is below the Z = 3 onset (5.65 eV). Ge at 1 keV light: +1.3 % (unweighted, includes low ω). Consistent with B1 §12 (≤ 10⁻⁴ in the plasmon window, percent-level at low ω).

**Cherenkov pole.** ε₁(0,ω) > 1 only for ω ≲ 4.5 eV in Si, and there ε₂ is large above 3.3 eV, so ω²ε_T = Q² is not reached sharply above the ionisation onset. Scan η = 0, 0.01, 0.1 eV over 4–24 eV: transverse/density 1.0054, 1.0054, 1.0057. No sensitivity.

## 6. Errors found in the spec and in the source
1. **CW4 §18 "the limit moves by the inverse".** Wrong for CRDM: the flux is itself ∝ σ̄, so N ∝ σ̄² and the limit moves by (R_URF/R_src)^{-1/2}. This holds if only the detection vertex changes, as here.
2. **CW4 §18 "weight near ω/Q → v_χ decides"** is right, and the weight is a pole, not a smooth weight: ⟨(1 − ω²/Q²)²⟩_src depends on the flux cut-off. The bound [(1 − v²)², 1] stays true and stays vacuous.
3. **"3-D (Q, ω, flux angle) quadrature"**: for an isotropic ELF and flux the angle is done analytically by (S.14). No flux-angle integral is needed. An anisotropic flux was not treated (CRDM from the l.o.s. integral is taken isotropic by the source).
4. **"Their Fig. 3" acceptance at 5 %** is met for heavy only, and only with two unstated cuts.
5. **2401.11971 Fig. 3 right panel, heavy curve.** The "×10²" label is inconsistent with the left panel: Σ_Z R_Z(heavy, as labelled) ≈ 0.79 against ∫dR/dω (×10²) = 8.6, and my R_Z is high by a flat ×10.5. The label should read ×10¹ (or the curve is low by ×10). The light curve is consistent: Σ_Z R_Z = 3.9 against 3.9.
6. **2401.11971:** Q ≤ 5 keV (heavy) and the T range are not stated; the heavy-mediator limit depends on the Q cut (×1.6 in σ̄ at 1 keV between Q ≤ 5 keV and the full table).
7. CW4 §18 quotes the transverse term "per unit of the density normalisation" without the common factor; §1 above fixes it as (1 − ω²/Q²)²F_DM²(Q²−ω²) for any m_V.
