## 1. Quantity
σ̄_lim^URF(m_χ) = σ̄_lim^src(m_χ)·N_src/N_URF for the scattering reach (3 events, 1 kg·yr), and ε_lim^URF(m_A′) = ε_lim^src·(Γ_src/Γ_URF)^{1/2} for absorption. Rules swapped, nothing else:

| Rule | Denominator of the event integrand | Source |
|---|---|---|
| src | \|1 + P Σ_i q̂_i²v_{F,i}²/κ_ii\|² | 1910.02091 (3.14), physical sign, typo fixed |
| URF | \|q̂·κ·q̂ + P Σ_i q̂_i²v_{F,i}²\|² | WP4 [5.2] |
| URFo | as URF with the symmetric off-diagonal part of Table 2 | §7 |
| lead | (q̂·κ·q̂)² | WP4 [5.1] leading form |

P(s) = −π(q̃²) of (3.9), s = ω² − Σq_i²v_{F,i}². Absorption: Γ_src = (m/3)Σ_i a_iP″/\|1 + a_iP\|², a_i = v_{F,i}²/κ_ii; Γ_URF = (m/3)Σ_i v_{F,i}²P″/\|κ_ii + v_{F,i}²P\|² (WP0-v2 L9b).

## 2. Method
Exact reduction [D]. Writing E = √(k̃²+Δ²), E′ = √(k̃′²+Δ²), d³k̃ = 2πEE′dEdE′/Q̃ and ½(1 − (k̃·k̃′+Δ²)/EE′)EE′ = (Q̃² − (E−E′)²)/4, the k integral at fixed (q, ω = E+E′) is closed form:

  R = (g/ρ_T)(ρ_χ/m_χ)(σ̄_e/8πμ²) ∫d³q ∫dω F_DM²\|F_med\|² q⁻¹ W(Q̃,ω) g̃(ω/q + q/2m_χ, ψ),
  W = (Q̃²δ − δ³/3)/(16π²Q̃ v_xv_yv_z),  δ = min(Q̃β, 2E_Λ − ω),  β = √(1 − 4Δ²/(ω² − Q̃²)).

Without the cut-off, e²πgW/q² equals the printed Im part of (3.9) exactly, which checks the normalisation of the source's rate against its own π(q̃²). The velocity integral (2.19) is the Radon transform of the truncated Maxwellian, so g̃ depends on v_min − v_e cos ψ only (closed form). Day average from (2.20) with 24 phases; the modulation amplitude A is the 12 h window statistic of §5.1 of the source.

Quadrature: Gauss–Legendre in cos θ (32) × uniform φ (48) × log q (48) × ω (32). Doubling all of these changes N_src and N_URF by < 2×10⁻⁶ (ZrTe₅ at 50 keV, BNQ-TTF at 10 keV). The spec's Lebedev 590 was not used; the product rule is converged.

Published curves were read from the **vector** PDFs in the arXiv source (pdftocairo → SVG paths; code/DC-49/extract_published.py), not digitised from pixels.

Findings:
1. **Isotropic check of [5.3].** Yb₃PbO gives N_src/N_URF = 1832 = 42.8² at every mass, in the integrator, with |ε_r|² kept. CW4 (9.1) is CONFIRMED numerically.
2. **ZrTe₅ moves more than the isotropised ×640.** The full anisotropic factor is 2200–5700 (th.), 1500–3400 (exp.): 3.2–3.8 decades, not 2.8. The source's rate is carried by q̂ near x and z (large v_F), where κ is large; the URF rule suppresses those directions most. BNQ-TTF moves by ×102–110 (2.0 decades). On the published scale the best ZrTe₅ reach becomes ≈ 3×10⁻⁴⁰ cm² (th.) and 7×10⁻⁴⁰ cm² (exp.); BNQ-TTF ≈ 4×10⁻⁴³ cm² before the ρ_T question of §3.
3. **Daily modulation is rule dependent.** Because the URF rule reweights q̂, A changes qualitatively: ZrTe₅ (exp.) at 56 keV goes from 0.31 to 0.62, ZrTe₅ (th.) at 62 keV from 0.016 to 0.44. The near-cancellation of A (the source's "modulation flip") is absent for the URF rule: A_URF stays ≥ 0.17 up to 1 MeV. The source's shaded 3σ regions therefore do not carry over. For BNQ-TTF A falls by about a factor 2. *Reproduction of A:* against ZrTe5_details.pdf the exp. curve agrees to 5–9 % over 12–700 keV, which validates the frame of (2.20). The th. curve does **not**: my A_src is low by ×2 and its zero sits near 175 keV against ≈ 500 keV in the source (A is a near-cancellation there). So A_src for th. above 20 keV is not reproduced; A_URF for th. is not a cancellation but inherits that caveat.
4. The source's N_src also uses |1 + …|² with the screening it calls weak: N_src/N_bare = 0.83–0.93 (ZrTe₅), 0.009–0.017 (BNQ-TTF), 0.22–0.44 (Yb₃PbO). BNQ-TTF is at α̃ ~ 10 (the source's own footnote), so one-loop RPA is outside its range there; LE-102 (CW4 §6) applies and the BNQ-TTF numbers are not more than model numbers.

## 7. Off-diagonal κ_ij and the half-gap
Only the symmetric part of κ enters q̂·κ·q̂. Table 2 prints a non-symmetric tensor (for Yb₃PbO κ_xy = −12.38, κ_yx = 8.58; a cubic crystal should have none). Keeping the symmetric part changes N_src/N_URF by < 1 % in every case (1832 → 1840–1850 for Yb₃PbO; ZrTe₅ 0.1 %). Dropping it is safe. Δ is used as the half-gap (LE-106), confirmed by the absorption thresholds: 2Δ = 31.2, 23.5, 10, 38.8 meV match the vertical edges of Summary_absorption.pdf.

## 8. The 0.55–0.62 factor
CW4 §9 says it must not multiply (9.1), though the DC-49 row title lists it. CW4 is right: N_src/N_URF = κ² exactly with Re δε kept, so applying the factor would double count. What the computation adds is that 0.55–0.62 is not a material-independent number. The quantity it stands for, N_URF/N_lead, is 0.83–0.95 for ZrTe₅, 0.22–0.44 for Yb₃PbO, 0.007–0.015 for BNQ-TTF. For the 1708.08929 ideal parameters (κ = 40, v_F = 4×10⁻⁴, g = 2, Λ = 0.2 keV) it runs 0.45–0.77 with mass, gap and cutoff scheme (0.61–0.72 at 100 keV), so the WP4 range sits inside that band at those parameters only, while N_src/N_URF = 1600.0 throughout (code/DC-49/ideal_check.py). Any recomputation that uses the leading form [5.1] must carry the computed N_URF/N_lead, not a fixed 0.55–0.62.

1. **Row title vs spec.** DC-49 title lists "the 0.55–0.62 charge renormalisation"; CW4 §9 forbids it for this re-plot. The row title should drop it.
2. **CW4 §9 "On axis the factor is (ε_bg,ii)²"** is right but does not bound the event-integrated factor usefully; the realised value (×5.7×10³) sits between 431 and 9.5×10⁴ and is not ×640.
3. **CW4 (9.1) "×5.0 for ε_bg = 25.3"** is correct only for the isotropised model; the anisotropic ZrTe₅ absorption factor is 6.2–6.3.
4. **Source Tables 1–2 vs its figures:** §3 (four items). The ρ_T item is the one that changes a published reach by a large factor.
5. **Source Eq. (2.26)/(2.27) sign:** the printed m² + Π^{ii} with (3.13) gives anti-screening; the curves use the physical sign.
6. Halo constants of 1910.02091 are unstated, and my best set (v_esc = 600 km/s) is inferred, degenerate with v_e near threshold, and not a claim about the authors' code.
