## 1. Hypotheses and objects
H1: Born–Oppenheimer, harmonic phonons, linear coupling to O(g²). H2: KS reference, with the e–e interaction at RPA (Hartree, kernel v) or adiabatic TDDFT (K = v + f_xc) level; ladder/excitonic (direct W) kernels are excluded and declared [C]. H3: T ≪ E_g. H4: insulator. H5 (for the "window" statements only): (q,ω) lies in the D1 §5.4 window, so no KS pair of momentum q has energy ω.
- KS response matrix: χ_KS,GG'(K,ω). Test-electron dielectric matrix: ε̃ = 1 − Kχ_KS. In RPA, ε̃ = ε. The density response is χ = χ_KS ε̃⁻¹, with S = −2Im χ₀₀ and L = (e²/2q²)S (D1 N4/N6).
- Pair momentum and energy: P ≡ q+Q (unfolded) and ω_f ≡ ω ∓ ω_{Qν} (upper sign = emission). Final pair f = (c, k+P; v, k), with ρ_f(P+G) ≡ ρ_cv(k, P+G).
- Density-matrix (TDH) perturbation theory [D, standard]. A one-body field F e^{−iω_i t} gives r^{(i)}_{ab} = F_{ab}(f_b − f_a)/(ω_i − ε_a + ε_b + i0). At second order, (ω_f − ε_a + ε_b)s_{ab} = N_{ab} + δV^{(2)}_{ab}(f_b − f_a), with N = [F^{(1)}, r^{(2)}] + [F^{(2)}, r^{(1)}]. On shell the residue N_cv equals D1 eq. (1) with the unrestricted m sum: the m-occupied terms map onto the m-empty form when ω₁+ω₂ = ε_c − ε_v. This rederives the D1/D2 Pauli theorem.

## 2. Derivation at RPA/TDDFT level, O(φ·g) amplitude [D under H1–H4]
Treat the DM potential φ_ext (at q, ω) and one phonon (Q ν, field g at frequency ω₂ = ∓ω_Q) as perturbations of TDH. Every self-consistent field is generated order by order.
1. **O(φ), channel q+G, frequency ω.** δV = Kχ_KS δV_eff, which gives V_eff(q+G) = ε̃⁻¹_{G0}(q,ω) φ_ext. The electrons feel the screened vertex ρ̃_mn(k,q) = Σ_G ε̃⁻¹_{G0}(q,ω) ρ_mn(k,q+G). R1 kept only the G=0 part, 1/ε(q,ω).
2. **O(g), channel Q+G, frequency ν.** This is the bare ion vertex dressed by all chains at (Q,ν), i.e. DFPT ΔV_SCF. Static DFPT replaces ε̃⁻¹(Q,ω_Q) by ε̃⁻¹(Q,0). In an insulator ε̃(Q,ν) is even and analytic in ν for \|ν\| < E_g, so the error is **O(ω_Q²/E_g²) ≈ 3×10⁻³** (sharper than D2's O(ω_Q/E_g)). The O(ω_Q/δ) non-adiabaticity of the amplitude is separate (D1 §4).
3. **O(φ g), channel P+G, frequency ω_f.** The second-order KS density at P+G,
   T_G(P) = (2/V) Σ_k Σ_{a,b} ⟨ψ_{b,k}|e^{−i(P+G)·r}|ψ_{a,k+P}⟩ N_ab(k)/(ω_f − ε_{a,k+P} + ε_{b,k} + i0), with F^{(1)} = ρ̃ and F^{(2)} = g,   (2.1)
   creates the field δV(P+G) = Σ_{G'} W_{GG'}(P,ω_f) T_{G'}, where W ≡ ε̃⁻¹(P,ω_f)K(P) is the screened kernel. This follows from δV = K(T + χ_KS δV). The sum in (2.1) runs over **all** band pairs (a,b), including the occ–occ and emp–emp second-order rearrangements, because s is the full second-order density matrix. Normalisation: g and M carry no 1/√N, as in D1 (1), and T is intensive.
4. **Transition amplitude** to f (the residue at the pair pole):
   **M̃_{f,±} = M^{(2)}_{±}[ρ̃] + Σ_{G,G'} ρ_f(P+G) W_{GG'}(P,ω_f) T_{G'}(P).**   (2.2)
   The first term is D1 eq. (1) with ρ → ρ̃. The second is the pair channel. K2 confirms that (2.2) equals the brute-force self-consistent TDH result exactly.
5. **Interpretation [D].** The e–h exchange kernel at pair momentum P is K_{ff'} = Σ_G ρ_f(P+G)K(P+G)ρ^*_{f'}(P+G). It is separable (rank N_G), so the Lippmann–Schwinger equation for the outgoing pair is solved exactly by the resolvent ε̃⁻¹(P,ω_f). That solution is (2.2). For P ≠ 0 the long-range G=0 term v(P) is **included**: the "macroscopic exclusion" of optics applies only to the external channel. This is what REF-B calls "exchange/local-field". In the frozen-phonon supercell, Q is a supercell reciprocal vector, so the term is literally a supercell local-field effect.
6. **Rate (unitarity) [D].** The proper Π^{(2)} contains the box (both g's on one loop, χ²) and the chain T̄WT. Im W = W^* Im χ_KS W, and the cut of T at the pair pole gives ρ^*M. Together these give −Im Π^{(2)}_{GG'} ∝ Σ_f M̃_f[G]M̃^*_f[G']δ. Under H5, ε̃⁻¹(q,ω) is non-absorptive (χ_KS(q,ω) has no anti-Hermitian part), so
   **L^{PA}(q,ω) = (8π²α/Ωq²) ⟨⟨Σ_{cvν±}(n_{Qν}+½±½)|M̃_±|² δ(ε_{c,k+P} − ε_{vk} ± ω_{Qν} − ω)⟩⟩ + O(g⁴).**   (2.3)
   The δ, Bose, spin and BZ factors are exactly those of D1 (4). Only the amplitude changes. W(P,ω_f) is complex, because pairs at P are open (they are the final states). Its Im part is part of the result.

A phonon line in place of the P-chain is O(g⁴). f_xc enters every channel through K. ALDA at \|P\| ≈ 1 Å⁻¹ gives \|f_xc/v(P)\| ≈ (P/2k_F)² ≈ 0.1 [E], a small correction to a correction.

## 4. (c) No double counting [D under H2 (RPA/ring level)]
Take a Coulomb-proper O(g²) diagram. Its nodes are fermion loops and the two bare ion vertices. Its edges are Coulomb lines, the phonon line, and the two external vertices, which sit on loops.
- At ring level each Coulomb line joins two distinct loops. A loop with a single Coulomb attachment and no other vertex is a G=0 tadpole and vanishes by neutrality. So the graph with the phonon line removed is a tree.
- Adding the phonon line closes **exactly one cycle**. A Coulomb line off the cycle is a bridge, so cutting it disconnects the diagram. This contradicts properness unless the bridge leads to an external vertex. Those are exactly the improper (q,ω) chains, which are excluded from Π and resummed once into ε̃⁻¹(q,ω).
- The cycle is: ion vertex → [chain at (Q,ν)] → loop L₁ → [chain] → loop L₂ → [chain at (Q,ν)] → ion vertex → phonon line. L₁ and L₂ carry the external vertices.
  - If L₁ = L₂, this is the box (χ², including the SE and V diagrams of D2). The only chains are the two (Q,ν) dressings.
  - If L₁ ≠ L₂, each is a triangle (ρ, g, Coulomb), and the chain between them carries (P, ω∓ν). This is the pair channel.
- The two segments adjacent to the ion vertices make up the DFPT dressing (all chains between a bare ion vertex and the first loop carrying ρ). The middle segment is W(P). Every Coulomb line therefore sits in exactly one of: the external bridge, a DFPT segment, or the pair segment.
- The classification is **topological** (the position of the line relative to the phonon vertices and the external vertices). It does not rely on q, Q and P being distinct mod G. At q→0 (optics) P = Q, but the DFPT segment and the pair segment are different edges, at ν≈0 and ω_f ≈ eV respectively. At Q→0, P→q, but the pair segment lies on the cycle while the external chain is a bridge.
- Hence "DFPT g + external ε̃⁻¹(q,ω) + pair W(P,ω_f)" sums each RPA diagram exactly once. Dropping W is an omission, not a cure for a double count.
- The rigid limit (§5) shows the REF-A worry "1/|ε|⁴" does not arise: the pair term partially cancels the 1/ε(q,ω) instead of multiplying it.

## 5. (d) The rigid-shift limit, solved exactly, and REF-A's claim
**Frame theorem [D; exact for the many-body Hamiltonian and for TDH/RPA].** Hypotheses:
- (i) The coupling has the commutator form g = u[A, H_KS] with A anti-Hermitian. For the Q=0 rigid translation, A = −iℓ û·P_e, the ASR form used by D1, D2 and REF-A.
- (ii) [n_q, A] = c n_q with c = iℓ û·q.
- (iii) A commutes with the e–e interaction. This holds for Coulomb by translation invariance, and also for the Hartree mean field (checked: W^† δV_H W = δV_H + O(u²)).

Transform with 𝒲 = e^{u(t)A}. The lab Hamiltonian H + u(t)[A,H] + φ n_q e^{−iωt} becomes H + φ n_q(1 + c u(t))e^{−iωt} − i u̇ A + O(u²). Hence, exactly:
- **(R1) Second-order response:** χ^{(2)} = c φ u [χ(q,ω_f) − χ(q,ω)] + D, where χ = χ_KS/ε̃ is the full response and D is the response to (φn_q at ω, −ω₂uA at ω₂). By K5, ε(ω)ε(ω_f)[χ(ω)−χ(ω_f)] = χ⁰(ω)−χ⁰(ω_f), so χ^{(2)} = T/(ε(q,ω)ε(q,ω_f)). The pair term is therefore indispensable even at Q→0.
- **(R2) Amplitude to f, by the residue at ω_f:** M̃_f = φ c ρ_f(q)·[1/ε(q,ω_f)] + D_f. Here D_f is the intraband (A_cc − A_vv), i.e. ℓû·m_eΔv, part found by D2/REF-A. It is screened at the vertex by ε(q,ω) and carries its own pair-channel feedback.
  - With the quantum phonon, the convective part gives exactly REF-A's |q·u|² S(q, ω−ω_Q) ∝ ε₂/|ε(q,ω_f)|². The D_f part is the p_u·P_e/M non-adiabatic term, which REF-A omits.
- **Cancellation mechanism [D].** In the rigid case the KS triangle is T = c[χ⁰(q,ω_f) − χ⁰(q,ω)] + T_D. The −χ⁰(q,ω) piece is the convective displacement of the first-order induced density, which is REF-A's anticipated "displaced induced charge". It cancels the pair-channel enhancement. So off shell T = O(ω₂). K4 checks this: T vanishes at ω₂ = 0 for g = [A,H] and stays O(1) for a generic g.
- **Sympy (exact, finite "crystal" with conserved momentum label).** K1 builds a model satisfying [X,A] = cX. K3 checks that the lab TDH and the frame TDH give identical second-order responses, and that the remainder after subtracting cuφ[χ(ω_f) − χ(ω)] is exactly the D-term T'/ε(ω_f).
  - Amplitude residues cannot be compared in a finite model, because the pair channel moves a discrete pole. (R2) rests on the analytic residue argument.

**Verdict on REF-A's claim:**
- (1) **Exact** for the convective/rigid component, at all orders in ω_Q/ω. There R1's 1/ε(q,ω) is wrong at first order: 1/ε(q,ω_f) = [1/ε(q,ω)]·[1 ± (ω_Q/ω)·∂lnε₁/∂lnω + O(ω_Q²/ω²)]. For Si (Penn-type ε₁, ∂lnε₁/∂lnω ≈ 0.14 at 1.3 eV, ω_Q/ω ≤ 0.053) this is ≤ 0.8% in amplitude and ≤ 1.5% in L [E].
- (2) **Not exact** for the intraband part D_f, which is screened at (q,ω) plus feedback.
- (3) **Wrong as a general prescription.** In the window the phonons are at generic, zone-boundary Q, and the rigid limit is kinematically closed (REF-A §4(iii)). The correction is then the non-rigid pair-channel term Λ (§6), which no frequency shift of ε(q,·) represents. The correct object is (2.2).

## 6. (e) When L ≈ ε₂^{PA}/ε₁^{(0)}(q,ω)² holds in the Si window (ω ≈ 1.2–1.5 eV, q ≈ 0.5–2 keV)
**Exact statement [D].** Under H5, (2.3) is exact to O(g⁴) at RPA level. The relation to the R1 form is
L^{PA} = [ε₂^{PA,R1}/ε₁^{(0)}(q,ω)²]·(1 + δ_LF + δ_pair + δ_ω + δ_ion) + O(g⁴), where:
- **δ_LF** (external local fields): the change from replacing ρ(q)/ε_M by Σ_G ε̃⁻¹_{G0}ρ(q+G). [E] 5–20%: about 10% at q ≲ 1 keV, by analogy with Si optical local fields. It grows towards q = 2 keV, as \|q+G\|_min = \|G₁₁₁−q\| decreases.
- **δ_pair** = 2Re⟨Λ⟩ + ⟨|Λ|²⟩, with Λ_f ≡ Σ_{GG'} ρ_f(P+G)W_{GG'}(P,ω_f)T_{G'}/M_f[ρ̃] and ⟨·⟩ the |M|²δ-weighted average over final states.
  - Split T = T_conv + T_def, where T_conv is the locally rigid (convective) part.
  - Λ_conv = ε(q,ω)/ε(q,ω_f) − 1 = O(ω_Q/ω), which is the δ_ω above.
  - Coherent-potential limit [D]: if M_{f'} = Uρ_{f'}(P) for all f' (the phonon acts as an effective local potential at P), then T = Uχ_KS(P) and **M̃ = M/ε(P,ω_f)** exactly, so Λ = 1/ε(P,ω_f) − 1.
  - In general, Λ_def ≈ (1/ε(P,ω_f) − 1)·η, where η ∈ [0,1] is a heuristic coherence factor measuring how much of the second-order induced density at P is non-convective, i.e. "Raman-like" ∂χ/∂u at finite Q [C for the bound].
  - **Si numbers [E].** The final pairs are band-edge to band-edge, so P ≡ k_c − k_v (mod G) ≈ 0.85·X (\|P\| ≈ 1 Å⁻¹) **independent of q**: the pair channel is the same as in indirect optical absorption (q=0, P=Q). ε(P≈1 Å⁻¹, ω_f ≈ 1.2 eV) ≈ 3–4, so the extreme bracket is \|1+Λ\|² ∈ [≈0.08, 1].
  - Optical calibration [E, from memory of Noffsinger 2012 / Zacharias–Giustino 2015, unverified here]: independent-particle phonon-assisted Si absorption, which has neither the pair channel nor excitons, matches experiment to within ≲ 2×. Hence \|1+Λ\|²×(excitonic factor) ≈ 0.5–2, and δ_pair ≈ −(10–50)% with sign [C] negative, as local fields in Si optics are.
- **δ_ω** ≈ ±1.5% (§5). The pair-channel frequency argument ω_f instead of ω inside W enters only through Λ, at O(ω_Q/ω).
- **δ_ion** ≲ 10⁻⁴ (§7).
- The ε₂²-term in |ε|² is O(g⁴) in the window (ε₂^{(1)} = 0), so H2 of D1 is redundant there (REF-B).

**Condition.** The simple form holds to O(ω_Q/ω) ≈ 5% (amplitude), i.e. ≈ 1–2% in L, **iff** \|Λ_def\| ≪ 1 and δ_LF ≪ 1. Equivalently: either the second-order induced density at P is convective (η ≪ 1), or \|P+G\|_min ≫ k_TF so that ε(P) → 1.
- Neither is guaranteed in Si. Without computing Λ, quote L^{PA} = ε₂^{PA}/ε₁² with a **systematic factor of [0.5, 1.2]** (pair channel and external local fields combined; excitons excluded) [E].
- A missing ingredient here would mean the [0.5, 1.2] systematic factor is too narrow. The test is the K-spec below: compute Λ at q = 0 and compare with optical data.

## 7. (f) Dark-photon (total-charge) coupling
The coupling is ρ_ch(q) = −e n_q + e Σ_{pκ} Z_κ e^{iq·(R_p+τ_κ+u_pκ)}.
- **Amplitude [D].** The ion vertex is O(u⁰) (Bragg, ω=0) or creates one phonon at Q ≡ q. Reaching (pair + phonon −Q) at O(e·g) then needs a g vertex that either annihilates that phonon (giving a zero-phonon final state) or adds one (two phonons). So no new PAER amplitude exists at O(e·g), confirming D2.
- **Quadratic coupling [D].** The path ion vertex → g^{(2)} converting phonon q into (pair + phonon −Q) has relative amplitude ~ Z q ℓ·g/ω. It is O(g²) in amplitude, so O(g⁴) in rate, beyond order.
- **Screening [D].** By the FDT for total charge, the rate uses L_tot = Im[−1/ε_tot]. The ionic polarisability enters in two places:
  - (i) the external channel, ε_tot(q,ω) = ε̃_el + ε_ion with ε_ion from the X diagram;
  - (ii) the pair-channel resolvent W(P,ω_f).
  The electron-only coupling also contains (i) and (ii) via the phonon-mediated interaction (D1 H1), so the two couplings differ only in the vertex (bare ion charge), and that vertex creates no PAER final state.
- **Si [E].** Z*(0) = 0. The finite-q dynamical charge is Z*(q) ~ O(qa) (quadrupoles). An ionic plasma scale Ω_ion ≈ ω_p·[(Z²/4)(m_e/M_Si)]^{1/2} ≈ 16.6 eV × 2.2×10⁻³ ≈ 37 meV for Z_eff ≈ 1 gives \|ε_ion/ε₁\| ≲ (Ω_ion/ω)²/ε₁ ≈ 7×10⁻⁵ at 1.3 eV, smaller still with Z*(q) < 1. The same factor applies in W(P,ω_f). **Negligible.**
  - Polar targets: ε_ion ≈ −(ε₀−ε_∞)ω_TO²/ω² ~ 10⁻³ absolute (REF-B), still negligible in the PAER window.

## 8. Code-usable recipe (b) and approximation level
**Route P (primitive cell, sum over states or Sternheimer):**
- (1) In the window, compute ε̃⁻¹_{G0}(q,ω) (Hermitian; G up to \|q+G\| ≲ q + 2 G₁₁₁). Form ρ̃.
- (2) M^{(2)}[ρ̃] from D1 eq. (1), with the unrestricted m sum and the full (A,B) paths.
- (3) For each (Q,ν,±), compute the triangle T_G(P) by (2.1) or, better, by a frequency-dependent **second-order Sternheimer** (mixed derivative ∂²n/∂φ_q∂u_{Qν} at (ω, ∓ω_Q), of the kind used for DFPT Raman tensors).
- (4) Compute W(P,ω_f) = ε̃⁻¹(P,ω_f)K(P), complex, at P = q+Q, using the same G set.
- (5) Form M̃ by (2.2) and insert into (2.3).
- Cost: T adds one full-BZ second-order response per (q,Q,ν). Because P is pinned near the indirect pocket momentum, W(P,ω_f) is needed only for a small set of P [E].

**Route S (ZG / frozen-phonon supercell; recommended):** L(q,ω) = ⟨Im[−ε̃⁻¹_{sc}]_{q,q}⟩_ZG (unfolded), with **RPA local fields in the supercell G-basis**, which contains every Q+G.
- The pair channel and the external local fields are then automatic. The adiabatic error in the pair-channel frequency is O(ω_Q/ω), on top of D1's O(ω_Q/δ).
- The R1 level is the same calculation with independent particles plus a scalar external 1/ε. The difference between the two runs measures δ_LF + δ_pair directly.

**Approximation level:** RPA or adiabatic TDDFT on KS bands. O(g²), harmonic, BO. Static DFPT g (O(ω_Q²/E_g²) in the screening). No e–h ladder (excitons [C]).

## 9. Exactly solvable checks performed (code/X2/rpa_pair_channel.py)
- **K1.** Finite model with a conserved momentum label, X: sector 0→1, and A anti-Hermitian with [X,A] = cX (rigid-translation algebra), g = [A,H₀]. Passes.
- **K2.** Eq. (2.2), i.e. screened vertex plus W·T, equals brute-force self-consistent TDH to second order. Difference 0, as an exact rational function of ω₁, ω₂, λ.
- **K3.** Lab TDH equals frame TDH for the second-order induced density. The convective part is cuφ[χ(ω_f) − χ(ω)], screened at ω_f (REF-A). The remainder is exactly the D-term T'/ε(ω_f), and it vanishes at ω₂ = 0. All differences are 0.
- **K4.** The rigid triangle vanishes as ω₂ → 0 (rigid cancellation). A generic non-commutator g gives an O(1) triangle at ω₂ = 0, so the pair channel is unsuppressed.
- **K5.** Scalar identity ε(ω)ε(ω_f)[χ(ω) − χ(ω_f)] = χ⁰(ω) − χ⁰(ω_f). Passes.
- **Not checkable in a finite model:** on-shell residues, because a discrete pair coupled to its own Hartree channel loses its residue at the bare pole. The continuum statements (2.2) and (2.3) rest on the analytic residue/Lippmann–Schwinger argument (§2.5).
